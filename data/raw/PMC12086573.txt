RILP cleavage links an inflammatory state to enhanced tau propagation in a cell culture model of Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is characterized by the progressive spread of tau pathology throughout the brain. Inflammation has been demonstrated to be present in the disease state as well as changes in endocytic trafficking. Here we identify the Rab7 effector RILP, a protein at the intersection of inflammatory states and endocytic trafficking, as a novel player in tau propagation. We show that RILP is cleaved in AD brain and this cleavage correlates to increases in hyperphosphorylated tau. Cleavage can be induced in both BE(2) neuron-like cells as well as a microglia cell line when they are treated with the inflammatory mediators lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and ATP. This inflammatory state also enhances tau propagation between BE(2) cells, an effect that is mitigated by overexpressing a noncleavable RILP. Furthermore, microglial cells contribute to intercellular tau propagation through both the release of inflammation-associated factors and the direct uptake and secretion of tau, potentially via extracellular vesicles (EVs). In HMC3 microglial cells, RILP cleavage led to impaired tau degradation, increasing intracellular tau accumulation. Additionally, the RILP cleavage status influences EV secretion in microglia. These findings suggest that RILP cleavage alters the endocytic trafficking of tau causing increased cell-cell propagation in a cell-culture model of AD.

INTRODUCTION Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the presence of extracellular beta-amyloid plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) consisting of hyperphosphorylated tau. NFTs disrupt regular cellular processes in neurons and eventually result in neuron death. While beta-amyloid plaques can be observed in asymptomatic patients, NFT load correlates with cognitive decline ( Hansen et al. , 2018 ; Kinney et al. , 2018 ; Thal and Tomé, 2022 ). Therefore, it is important to understand the role of tau in AD as it may lead to a better understanding of AD progression. It is well known that tau propagation follows a stereotypical pattern during AD progression. Propagation of tau pathology and elevated tau burden are strong predictors of cognitive decline; however, the mechanisms driving tau propagation are largely unknown. It is known that neurons secrete and uptake tau through several mechanisms. Tau has been demonstrated to be released from neurons in extracellular vesicles (EVs) and as a free protein ( Odfalk et al. , 2022 ; Langworth-Green et al. , 2023 ). In addition to plaques and NFTs, AD is characterized by chronic neuroinflammation due to activated microglia ( Kinney et al. , 2018 ). Microglia, the resident macrophages in the brain, are usually in a resting state but become activated in response to insults. It is thought that microglia become activated during AD due to the presence of β-amyloid and pathogenic tau. For example, it has been reported that uptake of tau aggregates activates the NLRP3 inflammasome in microglia ( Naseri et al. , 2019 ). While inflammation is protective as an acute response, it becomes harmful when it becomes chronic. The sustained immune response causes secretion of inflammatory factors that promote neuroinflammation and damage neurons. Neuroinflammation correlates with neurodegeneration and seems to enhance amyloid and tau pathology. Microglia have been reported to be directly involved in cell-to-cell tau spread by secreting tau aggregates ( Odfalk et al. , 2022 ). Disruptions in endolysosomal trafficking have long been thought to be involved in AD. Recent genome-wide association studies (GWAS) studies identified that several mis-regulated genes in AD are involved in endolysosomal trafficking. These genes, including SORL1 and BIN1, contribute to formation of β-amyloid and alter the degradation and secretion of tau ( Szabo et al. , 2022 ). RILP (Rab-interacting lysosomal protein) is a Rab7 adapter protein involved in endocytic trafficking. It is responsible for the retrograde trafficking of Rab7 containing vesicles by recruiting dynein ( Jordens et al. , 2001 ). Our lab discovered that RILP is cleaved during conditions of inflammation ( Wozniak et al. , 2016 ; Wozniak et al. , 2020 ; Ghosh et al. , 2023 ). Cleaved RILP (cRILP) causes vesicles to redistribute from the perinuclear region to the periphery ( Adams et al. , 2018 ). We have also shown that RILP cleavage results in increased exosome release as well as differential cargo loading ( Wozniak et al. , 2020 ). Because RILP cleavage is a consequence of inflammation and results in altered endocytic trafficking, we sought to determine whether this represents a phenomenon that could be occurring in AD and potentially contributing to tau propagation. We found that RILP cleavage is up-regulated during AD. This cleavage can be recapitulated in BE(2)-M17 neuroblastoma cells during a LPS/ATP-induced inflammatory stimuli. While LPS/ATP increased BE(2)-to-BE(2) tau propagation, this effect was ameliorated by overexpressing a noncleavable form of RILP (ncRILP). Additionally, we report that microglial cells enhance intercellular BE(2) tau propagation through both secreted inflammation-associated factors as well as direct uptake and secretion of tau potentially through EVs. RILP cleavage in HMC3 microglial cells results in decreased tau degradation, thus increasing tau burden. Additionally, the RILP cleavage status in microglia affects EV secretion. Overall, the data suggest that RILP, a protein at the intersection of endocytic trafficking and inflammatory states, is directly involved in tau propagation in a cell-based model of AD. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION RILP is cleaved in AD We first sought to determine whether RILP is cleaved during AD. Western blot analysis of total brain lysate from patients with AD and non-AD shows that RILP cleavage is up-regulated in the disease state ( Figure 1, A and C ). One of the major cell types implicated in AD tau pathology are neurons ( Odfalk et al. , 2022 ). Therefore, we first investigated whether RILP cleavage occurs in this specific cell type. In these studies, we chose to use undifferentiated BE(2)-M17 neuroblastoma cells. We chose this for several reasons namely because they allow us to better isolate and study the consequences of treatment with inflammatory mediators on tau trafficking, and they are more amenable to transfection, allowing us to specifically study the hyperphosphorylated tau variant via overexpression ( Chung et al. , 2001 ; Cetin et al. , 2022 ; Kim et al. , 2024 ). We also chose to use LPS/ATP treatment as our stimuli because it robustly activates the NLRP3 inflammasome. This inflammasome is not only reported to be activated in AD ( Heneka et al. , 2013 ) but is also the upstream component of RILP cleavage. Because RILP is a target of caspase-1, its cleavage is a direct consequence of inflammation ( Adams et al. , 2018 ). BE(2) cells were treated with 1 µg/ml LPS (lipopolysaccharide) for 3 h followed by 2.5 mM ATP for an additional hour. Successful stimulation by LPS/ATP is noted by increased IL1β, TNFα, caspase-1, and NLRP3 mRNA (Supplemental Figure S1A). Immunoblotting of whole-cell lysates shows that RILP cleavage occurs, and it is up-regulated in response to an LPS/ATP-induced inflammatory state (Supplemental Figure S1, B and C). Overall, RILP cleavage is occurring in AD, and can be recapitulated in BE(2) neuroblastoma cells. RILP influences tau propagation in BE(2) cells We next examined the role of inflammatory states and RILP cleavage in tau propagation. Tau propagation was assessed by performing conditioned media transfer assays. In this assay, parental BE(2) cells were transfected with a GFP-tagged phosphomimetic tau (GFP-tau(E14)). Tau-E14 was used because it mimics the hyperphosphorylated, pathological tau seen in AD ( Hoover et al. , 2010 ). After 24 h, the conditioned media was used to treat naïve BE(2) receiver cells. Uptake in the receiver cells is noted by the presence of GFP ( Figure 2A ). Western blot analysis reveals that tau(E14) is transferred to naïve BE(2) cells through conditioned media, and that transfer increases during LPS/ATP treatment ( Figure 2, B and D ). Astonishingly, the increase in transfer seen after LPS/ATP treatment is abrogated by inhibiting RILP cleavage via the expression of a dominant-negative, ncRILP in the parent cells ( Figure 2, C and D ) ( Wozniak et al. , 2016 ). We further found that not only is tau(E14) present in BE(2)-derived EVs, but this presence is diminished when ncRILP is expressed, thus representing a possible mechanism of transfer ( Figure 2, E and F ). Taken together, the data show that BE(2)-to-BE(2) tau propagation is enhanced in response to LPS/ATP. This occurs in a RILP-dependent manner and EVs represent one potential mechanism of spread. Microglia exacerbate tau transfer during inflammatory states Because microglia are the inflammatory cells of the brain and have been implicated in tau propagation ( Odfalk et al. , 2022 ), we next examined their effect on BE(2)-to-BE(2) tau transfer. Tau propagation was assessed by performing a conditioned media transfer whereby parental BE(2) cells expressing GFP-tau(E14) were coplated with HMC3 cells, a microglia cell line ( Figure 3A ). When indicated the coculture was treated with LPS/ATP. The cells were washed and incubated with fresh media for 24 h. The conditioned media was collected and used to inoculate a naïve set of BE(2) receiver cells. Uptake of tau in the receiver cells was assessed by the presence of GFP. As seen previously, LPS/ATP treatment promotes the transfer of tau(E14) from BE(2) to BE(2) ( Figure 3B ). Surprisingly, the presence of HMC3 microglial cells after LPS/ATP treatment dramatically increases tau(E14) transfer ( Figure 3, B and C ). The mechanism by which HMC3 microglia increase transfer in this system is not clear. We reasoned that two possibilities exist: 1) signals secreted from microglia during an inflammatory state act on a receiving cell to further promote inflammatory states and tau spread, or 2) microglia affect transfer through direct uptake and secretion of tau. To assess whether inflammation-associated factors secreted from microglia are sufficient to promote increased BE(2)-to-BE(2) tau propagation, we again performed a series of conditioned media transfers ( Figure 4A ). HMC3 microglia cells were treated with LPS/ATP. The cells were washed and incubated with fresh media for 24 h. The microglia-derived conditioned media was collected and used to inoculate a naïve set of GFP-tau(E14)-expressing BE(2) receiver cells. After 24 h, this conditioned media was collected and used to inoculate a second set of naïve BE(2) receiver cells. The presence of GFP-tau(E14) in this final set of cells was noted by Western blot for GFP. At baseline, HMC3 microglia are able induce the transfer of a small amount of GFP-tau(E14) from one BE(2) to another ( Figure 4, A and B ). However, the conditioned media from LPS/ATP-treated microglia dramatically increases the secretion and transfer of GFP-tau(E14) between BE(2) cells. This suggests that soluble factors secreted by LPS/ATP-treated microglial cells are sufficient to cause increased BE(2)-to-BE(2) transfer of tau(E14). We first assessed whether microglia could internalize tau. BE(2) cells were transfected with GFP-tau(E14). After 24 h, the media was collected and used to inoculate naïve HMC3 microglia. Western blot analysis of HMC3 cell lysates for GFP reveals that BE(2)-derived GFP-tau(E14) is taken up by microglia within 1 h (Supplemental Figure S2, A and C). Uptake is enhanced when the parent BE(2) cells are treated with LPS/ATP (Supplemental Figure S2, B and C). To determine whether microglia are secreting their internalized tau, we used a neuroblastoma-microglia-neuroblastoma conditioned media transfer system ( Figure 4C ). Here, crude conditioned media was collected from BE(2) cells expressing GFP-tau(E14). This media was used to inoculate naïve HMC3 microglia that had been previously stimulated with LPS/ATP. At the time of inoculation with conditioned media, no LPS/ATP remained in the culture. After 24 h, the media was collected and used to inoculate a final set of naïve BE(2)cells. The transfer of tau(E14) to these cells was evaluated by Western blot for GFP and shows that while both untreated and LPS/ATP-treated microglia take up tau(E14) ( Figure 4C , middle panel), only the treated microglia secrete it to a receiving BE(2) cell ( Figures 4C , right panel and D ). Together, these data suggest that microglia enhance tau propagation through direct uptake and eventual secretion, and inflammatory states exacerbate this process. RILP cleavage in microglia impacts endolysosomal trafficking Aberrant tau has been reported to induce inflammation in microglia ( Dutta et al. , 2023 ). Supporting this, overexpressed tau(E14) in microglia cells led to the up-regulation of several inflammation-associated markers including components of the inflammasome such as caspase-1 and IL1β (Supplemental Figure S3, A and B). We next assessed whether tau uptake itself can induce an inflammatory state. We performed a conditioned media transfer whereby media from HMC3 microglia cells expressing GFP or GFP-tau(E14) was used to inoculate naïve HMC3 receiver cells. No LPS or ATP was used. RT-PCR analysis of the receiver cells shows that tau(E14) internalization induces an inflammatory state (Supplemental Figure S3C). In previous work, we show that RILP is cleaved by caspase-1, a component of the active inflammasome ( Adams et al. , 2018 ). RILP cleavage reroutes endocytic vesicular trafficking away from the lysosome and instead the late endosome/multivesicular body moves toward plasma membrane to secrete its contents ( Cantalupo et al. , 2001 ; Wozniak et al. , 2010 ; Wozniak et al. , 2016 ; Wozniak et al. , 2020 ; Ghosh et al. , 2023 ). Given that microglia seem to be affecting tau propagation in response to LPS/ATP, we further sought to determine whether RILP could be involved in this phenomenon. Western blot analysis of HMC3 microglial cells shows that RILP is cleaved in these cells and that cleavage is up-regulated in response to LPS/ATP (Supplemental Figure S3, D and E). Because RILP is responsible for retrograde transport of organelles to the lysosome and the truncated form has been reported to inhibit degradation ( Cantalupo et al. , 2001 ), we asked whether RILP cleavage affects the degradation of tau. Cycloheximide was added to HMC3 microglial cells expressing GFP-tau(E14) with or without cRILP overexpression. The degradation of tau was observed under control conditions, but cRILP expression inhibited tau(E14) degradation resulting in the accumulation of intracellular tau ( Figure 5, A and B ). We previously showed a direct correlation between delayed protein degradation and an enhanced secretion of those proteins ( Wozniak et al. , 2010 ). We further showed that the RILP cleavage status directly affects macrophage EV secretion, with cRILP promoting EV secretion and ncRILP blocking it ( Wozniak et al. , 2020 ; Ghosh et al. , 2023 ). Here we find that this effect is also seen in HMC3 microglia whereby the presence of cRILP significantly increases EV secretion (Supplemental Figure S3F). Similar to when cells are treated with LPS/ATP ( Wozniak et al. , 2020 ; Ghosh et al. , 2023 ), the EVs secreted when RILP is cleaved are enriched in small EV markers including CD63, Tsg101, and CD9 (Supplemental Figure S3, G and H). Finally, we examined the effect of inflammatory states on EV-mediated tau secretion. HMC3 cells expressing GFP-tau(E14) were treated with LPS/ATP. EVs were purified from the culture supernatant via differential centrifugation. Figures 5, C and D show that tau(E14) is enriched in the EVs, and this enrichment is enhanced after treatment with LPS/ATP. Even more, blocking RILP cleavage via the expression of a noncleavable RILP, ncRILP, blocks EV-mediated tau(E14) secretion after LPS/ATP treatment. The above data show that during inflammatory states, RILP cleavage is enhanced in HMC3 microglia resulting in decreased tau(E14) degradation and increased secretion in EVs. To support this, we inhibited EV secretion via knockdown of Rab27 (Supplemental Figure S3I) ( Ostrowski et al. , 2010 ) and performed a conditioned media transfer ( Figure 5E ). Parent HMC3 microglial cells were transfected with shRNA to Rab27 and treated with LPS/ATP. Conditioned media from these cells was then used to inoculate naïve BE(2) receiver cells. The presence of GFP-tau(E14) in the receiver cells was noted by Western blot for GFP. Uptake of tau(E14) was dramatically reduced when Rab27 was silenced in parental HMC3 microglia ( Figure 5, E and F ). Silencing Rab27 not only reduced the amount of GFP-tau(E14) secreted into the media (Supplemental Figure S3J), but also significantly reduced the amount of tau(E14) present in EVs. Western blot analysis of EVs purified from the culture supernatants of shRab27 HMC3 cells shows that while EVs purified from LPS/ATP-treated cells are enriched in tau(E14), this enrichment is lost when Rab27 is silenced ( Figure 5, G and H ). Altogether the data reveal that RILP cleavage in HMC3 microglial cells disrupts endolysosomal trafficking, reduces tau(E14) degradation, and promotes the release of tau(E14)-enriched EVs, highlighting a RILP-dependent mechanism for tau propagation in a cell-based AD model. Alzheimer's disease is a complex neurodegenerative disorder characterized by amyloid plaques, tauopathy, and neuroinflammation. While the mechanism of tau propagation across brain regions is not fully understood, this study reveals the critical role of RILP, a key component of Rab7-mediated endolysosomal trafficking, in cell-to-cell tau transfer. We found that RILP is cleaved in the Alzheimer's brain. Cleavage is a direct consequence of inflammatory states as BE(2) neuroblastoma cells exhibit enhanced RILP cleavage during LPS/ATP treatment. Cleavage appears to enhance neuroblastoma-to-neuroblastoma tau propagation and blocking RILP cleavage inhibits this effect, suggesting a direct link between RILP and tau spread. We further explore the role of microglia in tau propagation, demonstrating that HMC3 microglial cells can enhance tau transfer through the secretion of inflammation-associated mediators as well as via the direct uptake and secretion of tau. RILP cleavage in LPS/ATP-treated microglial cells was found to inhibit tau degradation, and increase tau secretion in EVs, potentially contributing to tau spread. We show that tau is present in EVs derived from both BE(2) neuroblasts and HMC3 microglia; however, tau has also been reported to propagate via vesicle-free pathways ( Chai et al. , 2012 ; Polanco et al. , 2021 ). Yet, our findings suggest that RILP cleavage significantly influences EV secretion in microglia, and our Rab27 knockout experiments indicate a major role for EVs in tau transfer. However, the relative contribution of vesicular versus soluble protein mechanisms warrants further investigation. Overall, this work highlights RILP cleavage as a key mediator of inflammatory state–induced tau propagation in a cell-culture model of AD and opens new avenues for potential therapeutic interventions. Western blotting Whole-cell lysates were prepared from cells lysed in 1x RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. For Western blot normalizations, band intensities were calculated using the LI-COR image studio software. For ponceau, the intensity of the entire lane was used. To confirm the use of ponceau as a normalization control, band intensities of GFP, GAPDH, and ponceau were calculated using the LI-COR image studio software.
