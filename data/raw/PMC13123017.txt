Clinical, neuroimaging, and biomarker profiling of four Alzheimer’s disease pedigrees caused by pathogenic APP variants

Background Current understanding of the fluid biomarker profile in early-onset Alzheimer’s disease (EOAD) associated with pathogenic APP variants remains limited. We characterized four EOAD pedigrees carrying pathogenic APP variants using clinical, neuroimaging, and biofluid biomarkers, comparing them with contemporaneous sporadic AD (SAD) to inform early detection. Methods Between December 2022 and December 2024, we enrolled 206 individuals from four APP -associated families harboring the D678G, V717I, or M722K variants; 92 individuals underwent genetic testing. Based on genotype and clinical status, participants were classified into three groups: symptomatic APP variant carriers (SMC; n = 11), asymptomatic APP variant carriers (AMC; n = 19), and cognitively normal non-carriers (NC; n = 62). In addition, 60 patients with sporadic Alzheimer’s disease and an age at onset of ≤ 65 years (SAD; sporadic EOAD) were included as a comparison group for fluid biomarker analyses. All participants included in the plasma analyses underwent clinical assessments, and plasma levels of P-tau217, P-tau181, Aβ42, Aβ40, GFAP, NfL, and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio were quantified. Structural MRI, PET, and cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers were obtained in a subset of participants, where available. Group comparisons were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney U tests, with false discovery rate adjustment for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure ( P _FDR). Additionally, two patients treated with lecanemab underwent longitudinal follow-up during the first 15 infusions. Results Based on family informant reports and clinical observations, the median age at onset (AAO) among affected individuals in these pedigrees was 48.50 (44.0, 51.8) years. Plasma biomarker analyses showed that P-tau181, P-tau217, and GFAP levels were significantly higher in the SMC and SAD groups than in the AMC and NC groups ( P _FDR &lt; 0.05). Compared with the SMC group, the SAD group exhibited higher Aβ40 levels and a lower Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio (P _FDR &lt; 0.05). Among V717I carriers, multiple biomarkers differed significantly between SMC and AMC ( P _FDR &lt; 0.05), whereas no significant differences were detected in the D678G or M722K subgroups. In addition, given the current sample size, APOE ε4 status was not associated with additional modification of plasma biomarker levels or AAO. No amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) were observed during the lecanemab treatment period. From the pre-infusion baseline to immediately before the 15th infusion, one patient showed decreases in P-tau181 (− 5.70%), P-tau217 (− 12.23%), and GFAP (− 42.93%), accompanied by a mild improvement in cognitive performance; the other patient showed a reduction in P-tau181 (− 28.30%) but increases in GFAP (+ 36.59%) and NfL (+ 174.01%), with overall stable cognition. Conclusions Plasma biomarkers provide critical evidence to advance our understanding of APP variant-associated EOAD. Both APP variant-associated EOAD and sporadic EOAD showed a similar biomarker profile, with higher plasma P-tau181, P-tau217, and GFAP levels than those in AMC and NC. In contrast, the two groups exhibited divergent patterns in Aβ40 levels and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio. In addition, given the current sample size, APOE ε4 status was not found to have an additional modifying effect on plasma biomarkers among APP variant carriers. Introduction Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is among the most common neurodegenerative disorders. Early-onset AD (EOAD) is typically defined by symptom onset before 65 years of age and accounts for approximately 5–10% of all AD cases [ 1 ]. In autosomal-dominant familial EOAD, pathogenic variants in APP , PSEN 1, and PSEN2 constitute the canonical genetic causes and are inherited in an autosomal-dominant pattern [ 2 ]. Among these, PSEN1 variants are the most frequent (70–80%), followed by APP variants (10–15%), whereas PSEN2 variants are relatively rare [ 3 ]. The APP gene is located on chromosome 21q21, and &gt; 70 pathogenic or likely pathogenic APP variants associated with familial AD have been reported to date [ 4 ]. Mechanistically, APP mutations can perturb β- and γ-secretase-mediated processing, leading to increased Aβ (amyloid-β) production or an altered Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio that promotes aggregation and deposition, thereby triggering downstream tau-related pathology and neurodegenerative cascades [ 5 ]. The pathological hallmarks of AD include extracellular accumulation of Aβ plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein [ 6 ]. Accumulating evidence indicates that cerebral Aβ deposition and tau-related changes can be detected years before symptom onset, suggesting that AD pathobiology begins in the preclinical stage [ 7 ]. Nevertheless, early clinical manifestations are often subtle and complex to identify. In recent years, the expanding use of fluid and imaging biomarkers to assess Aβ, tau pathology, and neurodegeneration has shifted AD diagnosis from a phenotype-driven approach toward an integrated framework that combines clinical assessment with biological evidence [ 8 ]. For individuals with suspected AD, a multimodal evaluation incorporating neuropsychological testing, structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), positron emission tomography (PET), and fluid biomarkers can improve diagnostic accuracy and support biological stratification and staging [ 9 , 10 ]. EOAD associated with different APP mutations exhibits variability in clinical phenotype, neuroimaging involvement, and fluid biomarker profiles; phenotypic heterogeneity may also be observed within the same pedigree [ 11 ]. However, systematic investigations integrating multiple pedigrees and multidimensional measures remain scarce. Therefore, we enrolled four Chinese pedigrees carrying APP variants (D678G, V717I, and M722K). The V717I and M722K variants have been previously reported, whereas D678G represents a novel variant identified in the present study. We systematically characterized clinical features, neuroimaging findings, and fluid biomarkers to inform clinical identification and risk stratification. Materials and methods Participants From December 2022 to December 2024, we consecutively enrolled individuals presenting to the Department of Neurology at Henan Provincial People’s Hospital (Zhengzhou University People’s Hospital) who met clinical diagnostic criteria for Alzheimer’s disease (AD) or mild cognitive impairment due to AD (MCI due to AD). AD was diagnosed according to the 2011 National Institute on Aging-Alzheimer’s Association (NIA-AA) criteria, and MCI was defined using Petersen’s criteria. Individuals with suspected familial aggregation underwent genetic testing. Probands were screened by whole-exome sequencing (WES), and candidate variants were subsequently validated by Sanger sequencing in additional family members for within-family segregation analysis. This workflow identified four AD pedigrees carrying pathogenic APP variants (D678G, V717I, and M722K; pedigree charts are shown in Fig. 1 ). Clinical history, neurological examination findings, laboratory results, and ancillary test results were systematically collected for probands and their family members. Blue markers indicate individuals who provided blood samples and underwent genetic testing, green markers indicate individuals affected by Alzheimer’s disease, and red markers indicate variant carriers Based on genotype and clinical phenotype, family members were classified into three groups: (1) symptomatic APP variant carriers (SMC)-carriers of the target APP variant with cognitive decline meeting clinical criteria for AD or MCI due to AD; (2) asymptomatic APP variant carriers (AMC)-carriers of the target APP variant who were cognitively unimpaired; and (3) cognitively normal non-carriers (NC)-non-carriers within the pedigrees who were cognitively unimpaired. The group sizes were n = 11 (SMC), n = 19 (AMC), and n = 62 (NC). In parallel, we included a contemporaneous cohort of sporadic Alzheimer’s disease (SAD). To minimize confounding, we applied a stratified matching strategy with the SMC group as the reference. SAD participants were first stratified by Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR: 0.5, 1, 2, 3) and matched by sex within each stratum. Within the same CDR-sex stratum, individuals with the closest assessment age were prioritized; when necessary, years of education were additionally considered. A total of n = 60 SAD participants were included. Inclusion criteria for SAD were: (1) age at assessment ≤ 65 years; (2) Abeta-PET positivity; (3) a clinical diagnosis of AD or MCI according to NIA-AA criteria (with MCI stratified by CDR). Exclusion criteria were: (1) other neurodegenerative dementias (e.g.vascular dementia, frontotemporal dementia, dementia with Lewy bodies, and Parkinson’s disease dementia); (2) dementia due to traumatic brain injury, intracranial tumor, or central nervous system infection; (3) conditions potentially affecting cognition, including HIV or syphilis seropositivity, thyroid dysfunction, vitamin B12 deficiency, and alcohol or substance misuse. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital (Approval No. 202076). All participants (probands, asymptomatic carriers, cognitively normal non-carrier family members, and patients with sporadic AD) provided written informed consent before enrollment. Genetic testing was conducted within a standardized genetic counseling framework: certified genetic counselors provided counseling both before testing and during result disclosure, and participants could choose whether to be informed of their carrier status. After receiving a full explanation of the purpose and potential risks of lumbar puncture, participants provided consent and voluntarily underwent cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) collection. Clinical assessment Medical and family histories were obtained from caregivers or close relatives. All participants underwent a comprehensive clinical evaluation, including a general physical examination, neurological examination, and laboratory testing. Laboratory assessments included serum folate, vitamin B12, thyroid function, HIV serology, and syphilis serology. Probands and a subset of carriers additionally underwent neuroimaging, biomarker testing, and neuropsychological assessments. Neuroimaging comprised brain MRI, magnetic resonance angiography (MRA), ^18F-FDG-PET, Abeta-PET, and Tau-PET. Plasma and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) were collected for quantification of AD-related biomarkers. Neuropsychological assessments included the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), and the Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR). Genetic testing and analysis Probands with a positive family history underwent WES (Berry Genomics, Beijing, China) to screen for AD-related variants. To exclude the possibility that the disease was attributable to copy number variants (CNVs), such as APP duplication, we performed additional genome-wide CNV testing in the four probands alongside whole-exome sequencing. CNV-seq, a high-throughput sequencing-based approach for CNV detection, was used to screen for genome-wide copy number alterations, with a focus on detecting microduplications at the APP locus and other clinically relevant CNVs (Berry Genomics, Beijing, China). Family members requiring segregation testing underwent Sanger sequencing (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) to validate candidate variants. Fresh peripheral venous whole blood was collected, and genomic DNA was extracted using the EZup Column Blood Genomic DNA Extraction Kit (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China). After quality control, target regions were amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR). PCR products were assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis, purified by gel extraction, and sequenced on an ABI Prism 3730xl Genetic Analyzer (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Chromatograms were interpreted using Chromas software. Sequence alignment and variant annotation were performed against the UCSC hg19 human reference genome to determine genotypes. For the newly identified variant, population allele frequency was assessed by querying the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD) and the 1000 Genomes Project. Pathogenicity was evaluated using a combination of evolutionary conservation analyses and in silico functional predictions. Homologous amino acid sequences across species were retrieved from UniProt to assess evolutionary conservation. Multiple in silico tools were then applied to predict functional impact, including VarCards ( http://varcards.biols.ac.cn/ ), REVEL ( https://sites.google.com/site/revelgenomics/༉ , MutationTaster ( https://www.mutationtaster.org/༉ , and PolyPhen-2( https://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/༉ . Plasma and CSF biomarker measurements Blood samples were collected from 92 individuals across the four pedigrees (SMC, n = 11; AMC, n = 19; NC, n = 62). CSF was collected from five individuals [SMC, n = 2 (F1:IV 9 , F2: II 3 ); AMC, n = 3 (F1:III 20 , F1:IV 20 , F1:IV 32 )]. Two probands (F1: IV 9 and F2: II 3 ) received lecanemab for 15 infusions, with plasma collected pre-infusion for biomarker testing. All 60 SAD participants also provided peripheral blood samples. Biospecimens were collected and processed using a standardized protocol. Fasting peripheral venous blood was drawn in the morning into EDTA tubes, stored at 4 °C, and centrifuged within 2 h (1,500×g for 10 min). Plasma was aliquoted and stored at − 80 °C. Frozen samples were thawed only once before assay to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. CSF was obtained by lumbar puncture and collected into sterile polypropylene tubes; samples were centrifuged promptly to remove cellular components according to standard operating procedures, aliquoted, and stored at − 80 °C, with a single thaw before testing. Plasma and CSF AD-related biomarkers were quantified using a chemiluminescence immunoassay platform (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Analytes included phosphorylated tau (P-tau181 and P-tau217), Aβ42, Aβ40, glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), and neurofilament light chain (NfL). All assays were performed in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions and quality-control procedures. Reference thresholds for plasma and CSF are provided in Supplementary Table 1. Neuroimaging Among family members, 14 underwent brain MRI (SMC, n = 9; AMC, n = 5), 9 underwent ^18F-FDG-PET (SMC, n = 4; AMC, n = 5), 10 underwent Abeta-PET (SMC, n = 5; AMC, n = 5), and 2 underwent Tau-PET (both in SMC). All 60 SAD participants underwent Abeta-PET. PET images were independently interpreted by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians blinded to clinical data, according to internationally accepted visual read criteria; a third senior nuclear medicine physician adjudicated discrepancies. Cortical standardized uptake value ratios (SUVRs) were calculated using cerebellar gray matter as the reference region. APOE genotyping Peripheral venous whole blood was collected, and genomic DNA was extracted using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (QIAGEN). APOE genotyping targeted rs429358 and rs7412 and was performed using TaqMan probe-based allelic discrimination on a QuantStudio™ 12 K Flex Real-Time PCR System. Reaction mixtures and cycling conditions were set according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Negative and positive controls were included, and a subset of samples was retested to assess concordance. To investigate the presence of potentially protective APOE variants (R136S, V236E, and R251G), all APP variant carriers ( n = 30) underwent additional resequencing of APOE exons. Three primer pairs were used to cover all four exons; primers were designed using Primer-BLAST, and their specificity was verified by sequence alignment. Nested PCR was performed when necessary to improve amplification efficiency. PCR was performed using an Applied Biosystems 9700 PCR System, and purified amplicons were subjected to Sanger sequencing on an ABI 3730XL DNA Analyzer. Chromatogram alignment and variant calling were performed using Mutation Surveyor, and chromatograms were also visualized in Chromas when required. Lecanemab treatment and safety monitoring After clinical staging, risk assessment, and exclusion of contraindications, two family cases (F1:IV 9 and F2:II 3 ) received lecanemab. Lecanemab was administered intravenously at a dose of 10 mg/kg every two weeks (q2w). Both individuals completed 15 infusions and were followed longitudinally from baseline for at least 6 months. Plasma was collected pre-infusion to measure P-tau217, P-tau181, Aβ42, Aβ40, GFAP, NfL, and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio. Safety monitoring focused on amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), with brain MRI performed at baseline and during treatment, as per the study protocol. Statistical analysis Continuous variables were non-normally distributed and were presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Overall comparisons across the four groups (SAD, SMC, AMC, and NC) were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis H test, followed by pairwise comparisons using the Mann-Whitney U test. Multiple testing was controlled using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure; FDR-adjusted P values were denoted as P _FDR. Categorical variables were presented as counts (%) and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. To quantify differences relative to NC and to report effect sizes, plasma biomarkers were log-transformed, and geometric mean ratios (GMRs) with 95% confidence intervals were calculated; forest plots were generated on the log scale. Stratified analyses were performed separately for each APP variant (D678G, V717I, and M722K). We prespecified three comparison frameworks: (1) each variant subgroup versus NC; (2) each variant subgroup versus SAD; and (3) within-variant comparisons of SMC versus AMC. All multiple comparisons were adjusted for the false discovery rate (FDR). For APOE -related analyses, two-sided Fisher’s exact tests were used to compare APOE ε4 carrier status and the distribution of major APOE genotypes between the SAD group and the APP variant carrier group, and odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. To evaluate the modifying effect of APOE ε4 on plasma biomarkers, participants were stratified into four genetic groups: non-SAD ε3/ε3 (reference group), SAD ε4 + (Group I), all APP variant carriers (Group II), and APP variant carriers with APOE ε4 (Group III). Overall group differences were assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis H test, followed by pairwise comparisons against the reference group using the Mann-Whitney U test, with FDR adjustment via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (adjusted P values denoted as P _FDR). In addition, linear regression models were fitted on the log scale, including terms for APP , ε4, and their interaction, with adjustment for age and sex; results were presented as geometric mean ratios (GMRs; GMR = exp[β]) with 95% CIs. Given the limited sample size in some subgroups, interaction effects were interpreted as exploratory. Age at onset (AAO) analyses included only symptomatic participants (SAD and SMC). Participants were categorized into four groups: SAD ε3/ε3 (reference group), SAD ε4 + (Group I), SMC (Group II), and SMC ε4 + (Group III). Group differences were evaluated using the Kruskal-Wallis test, and pairwise comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test with FDR adjustment ( P _FDR). The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) subgroup was small and was therefore analyzed only on an exploratory basis. Given the limited longitudinal lecanemab sample ( n = 2), these findings were considered case-level exploratory observations and were not used to infer cohort-level treatment effects. Longitudinal trends were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis. Individual-level z-scores [z = (x-the individual mean)/the personal standard deviation] were used to construct heatmaps visualizing within-person dynamic changes. All tests were two-sided, and P _FDR &lt; 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Discussion Clinical research in Alzheimer’s disease (AD) has become increasingly biomarker-driven; however, the biomarker natural history of EOAD-particularly in pedigrees carrying pathogenic APP variants-remains insufficiently characterized [ 12 ]. In families harboring pathogenic APP variants, we integrated genetic evidence, plasma biomarkers, PET-based staging, and longitudinal assessments before and after anti-Aβ disease-modifying therapy (DMT) to support early detection and risk stratification. In overall analyses, plasma P-tau181, P-tau217, GFAP, and NfL levels were higher in the SMC and SAD groups than in the NC and AMC groups. The SAD group showed the expected Aβ profile, with higher Aβ40 and lower Aβ42 and Aβ42/Aβ40. Compared with SMC, SAD had higher Aβ40 and lower Aβ42 and Aβ42/Aβ40, whereas P-tau, GFAP, and NfL did not differ significantly between the two groups [ 13 , 14 ]. We also evaluated APOE as a potential modifier; however, no significant modifying effect of APOE ε4 on the APP -associated plasma Aβ profiles was observed in this study. Compared with NC, the V717I-SMC subgroup showed elevated P-tau181, P-tau217, and GFAP levels; the D678G-SMC subgroup also showed higher P-tau217 levels; and the M722K-AMC subgroup showed higher P-tau217 and Aβ42 levels. Compared with SAD, APP variant carrier subgroups generally showed lower Aβ40 levels and a higher Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio; notably, the M722K-AMC subgroup had higher Aβ42 levels and a higher Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio than the SAD group. Variant-specific within-group comparisons further indicated that only in the V717I subgroup did SMC, relative to AMC, show older age at assessment and higher levels of P-tau181, P-tau217, and GFAP. In contrast, no consistent differences between SMC and AMC were observed within the D678G or M722K subgroups, potentially reflecting genotype-specific biology and limited sample size with imbalanced stage distributions [ 15 ].Notably, routine structural MRI was largely unremarkable in our cohort; in contrast, fluid biomarkers and PET suggested that AD pathophysiological processes had already begun. For example, although F1:IV 18 , F3:III 2 , and F3:III 3 were Abeta-PET positive, their plasma biomarkers did not exceed the prespecified positivity thresholds. Still, they remained elevated relative to age-matched non-carriers, underscoring the value of multimodal assessment for early identification and longitudinal monitoring [ 9 , 16 ]. From a genetic perspective, D678G (exon 16) and V717I/M722K (exon 17) are located within the Aβ-coding and proteolytic processing-related region of APP (exons 16–17), a mutational hotspot in familial Alzheimer’s disease [ 4 ]. APP V717I is a well-established pathogenic variant; since its first report by Goate and colleagues in 1991, multiple studies have supported its strong association with autosomal-dominant EOAD [ 17 ]. Nevertheless, we identified an asymptomatic 57-year-old carrier (F1:III 20 ) with no overt structural abnormalities on MRI and negative Aβ-PET and ^18F-FDG-PET (Fig. 2 C). This individual carried an APOE ε2/ε3 genotype. In contrast, the proband (F1:IV 9 ) was heterozygous for ε4. Prior evidence suggests a potential modifying role of APOE in autosomal-dominant AD: in the PSEN1 E280A kindred, ε2 has been associated with delayed onset. In some APP V717I pedigrees,ε2 has been linked to later-onset, whereasε4 may be related to earlier-onset [ 18 , 19 ]. However, the proband’s sister (F1:III 18 ), who also carried ε2/ε3, developed symptoms at 53 years of age and progressed to severe dementia, indicating substantial inter-individual variability in clinical expression even among carriers of the same pathogenic variant and APOE genotype. Full-length APOE sequencing did not detect any potentially protective APOE variants (R136S, V236E, or R251G), suggesting that other genetic modifiers, environmental exposures, or individual susceptibility may also influence inter-individual variability in later age at onset [ 20 ]. Regarding APP M722K, following the first report from China in 2015, we report an additional Chinese pedigree, further expanding the available pedigree-level and phenotypic evidence for this variant [ 21 ]. To date, D678G has not been publicly reported; however, other substitutions at the same residue (e.g.D678H and D678N) have been documented in the literature and/or databases [ 4 ]. In the D678G family, eight members underwent genetic testing. Both affected individuals were variant carriers, whereas two non-carriers and four carriers were currently unaffected, likely due to their young age. In addition, multiple in silico tools predicted a deleterious functional impact of this variant, and it was absent from public population databases, suggesting it is scarce. Together with the proband’s typical AD features-hippocampal atrophy and Abeta-PET positivity-these lines of evidence support D678G as a likely pathogenic candidate variant in this pedigree. Our findings show that plasma P-tau181, P-tau217, GFAP, and NfL levels were higher in the SMC and SAD groups than in the NC and AMC groups, suggesting that, upon reaching a clinically relevant stage, tau-related abnormalities, astroglial activation, and axonal injury exhibit broadly concordant, stage-associated changes in peripheral blood across both autosomal-dominant and sporadic AD [ 13 , 22 ]. Prior studies indicate that P-tau217 can rise during the preclinical phase and may index early tau burden; GFAP more closely reflects early astrocytic activation linked to Aβ deposition; and NfL, a relatively nonspecific injury marker, aligns more closely with neurodegeneration severity and the pace of disease progression [ 23 – 25 ]. In our cohort, the SAD group showed higher Aβ40, lower Aβ42, and a reduced Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio, with this Aβ pattern more pronounced than in SMC. Prior work has demonstrated that autosomal-dominant AD, particularly APP -related disease, can modify APP processing and alter the Aβ42/Aβ40 profile, such that it does not always follow the canonical decrease seen in sporadic AD, pointing to underlying biological differences between the two [ 26 , 27 ]. Although APOE ε4 is a significant genetic risk factor for sporadic AD, we did not observe a significant modifying effect on APP -associated plasma Aβ profiles or age at onset (AAO) in this study. Specifically, the ε4 carrier frequency was higher in SAD than in APP variant carriers (OR = 2.86; P = 0.06), yet the APP × APOE ε4 interaction term was not significant, and ε4 did not appear to accelerate AAO within SMC further, noting the minimal number of ε4 -positive SMC. Therefore, at the current sample size, the APP variant effect seems to predominate in shaping Aβ40 levels and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio. After stratification by variant, differences between SMC and AMC were not consistently detected in the M722K and D678G subgroups. This likely reflects smaller subgroup sizes with reduced power after multiple-comparison adjustment, uneven distributions of age and disease stage, and possible non-linear or plateau behavior of some biomarkers [ 28 ]. Even so, plasma biomarkers still appear to capture stage-related biological changes in pedigree-based cohorts, and the timing of key biomarker shifts may differ across APP variants. This idea will need to be tested in longitudinal follow-up studies [ 29 ]. With respect to cerebrospinal fluid, the canonical AD sequence typically involves an early decrease in Aβ42 and Aβ42/Aβ40, followed by an increase in P-tau; consistent with this cascade, SMC relative to AMC exhibited lower Aβ-related measures accompanied by higher P-tau, supporting ongoing AD pathophysiology [ 7 , 30 ]. Neuroimaging remains central to assessing neurodegeneration. In this study, structural MRI in most individuals from the four pedigrees did not show a typical AD atrophy pattern. At the same time, molecular imaging provided earlier diagnostic cues. This pattern is consistent with autosomal-dominant AD cohorts, in which amyloid pathology typically precedes metabolic disruption and structural atrophy emerges relatively later. Accordingly, in preclinical or very early stages, routine MRI may fail to show typical medial temporal/hippocampal-predominant atrophy; its primary utility lies in excluding comorbidities and establishing a structural baseline, rather than serving as the sole basis for early staging [ 10 , 31 ]. We performed Aβ-PET in 11 individuals (6 AD patients and 5 asymptomatic carriers). All AD patients were Aβ-PET–positive, supporting the role of Aβ-PET in providing in vivo evidence of amyloid pathology, even when structural changes are subtle, thereby contributing to early identification and risk stratification [ 32 ]. Plasma biomarker results were generally consistent with PET findings; however, three Aβ-PET-positive individuals (F1:IV 18 , F3:III 2 , and F3:III 3 ) had plasma P-tau217 values in the borderline range. This suggests that, in very early disease stages, PET evidence of amyloid pathology may emerge before plasma markers exceed predefined cutoffs, potentially reflecting inter-individual biological variability and assay-related variation. Thus, a negative or borderline single-analyte plasma result should not be interpreted as excluding cerebral amyloid deposition; an integrated interpretation that incorporates PET, clinical staging, and longitudinal follow-up is warranted [ 16 , 33 ]. The Tau-PET results presented in this study were available from only one pedigree ( n = 2), and both individuals carried the V717I variant; no Tau-PET data were available for the other pedigrees at the time of analysis. Tau-PET is primarily used to refine staging and aid interpretation of inter-individual differences [ 34 ]. At present, ^18F-FDG-PET data were available only for the family carrying the APP V717I variant; no ^18F-FDG-PET data were obtained for the other variant subgroups.^18F-FDG-PET was performed in nine individuals (4 AD patients and 5 asymptomatic carriers). All four AD patients showed hypometabolism in association cortices, predominantly involving the temporal and parietal lobes and the temporo-parieto-occipital junction, with potential extension to the posterior cingulate/precuneus and frontal regions; one patient also showed left caudate hypometabolism. Except for one carrier affected by trauma-related changes, asymptomatic carriers showed no apparent metabolic abnormalities, suggesting that FDG-PET more reliably detects AD-typical injury patterns during the symptomatic phase and may not be consistently positive in asymptomatic stages. Nonetheless, FDG-PET remains valuable for differential diagnosis across dementia syndromes and can complement plasma biomarkers, PET staging, and cognitive assessments within a multimodal framework [ 10 , 34 ]. Two probands received standardized lecanemab therapy and completed 15 infusions (every 2 weeks), with plasma biomarker measurements and cognitive assessments obtained before each injection. Over follow-up, F1:IV 9 showed a marked decline in GFAP and a downward trend in P-tau217, accompanied by modest improvements in MMSE and MoCA, and overall stability in CDR; F2:II 3 showed a decrease in P-tau181, along with declines in Aβ42 and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio, while cognitive measures remained broadly stable. Neither patient developed amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) during treatment. Given that only two treated individuals were included, these longitudinal findings should be interpreted as exploratory, case-level observations rather than cohort-level estimates of treatment effects. Randomized controlled trials have shown that lecanemab reduces cerebral amyloid burden and slows clinical decline; at the fluid biomarker level, downstream markers (P-tau and GFAP) often shift in a direction consistent with improvement, whereas neuronal injury markers such as NfL tend to respond more slowly and display substantial inter-individual variability [ 35 , 36 ]. In our study, the declines in P-tau181, P-tau217, and GFAP are concordant with prior reports, suggesting that upstream Aβ-targeted intervention may be accompanied by attenuation of downstream tau-related abnormalities and glial reactivity. The limited magnitude of change on cognitive scales is also consistent with existing evidence [ 37 ]. Given the small sample size, the absence of ARIA in our two cases should be interpreted as an individual-level observation rather than as evidence of population-level incidence [ 11 ]. Despite the comprehensive, multimodal characterization of biomarkers in APP -mutation pedigrees, several limitations merit consideration. First, our conclusions are primarily exploratory because APP -related AD is rare, and the sample size is small [ 12 ]. Second, we did not include comparative pedigrees with PSEN1 or PSEN2 variants, which limits cross-genotype inference. Third, only two individuals received lecanemab ( n = 2); therefore, the longitudinal fluid biomarker findings are descriptive, case-level observations that may be influenced by inter-individual variability and sampling timing and should not be extrapolated to infer population-level treatment effects. Future work should therefore extend to multicenter cohorts with larger samples and longer follow-up to delineate biomarker natural history more definitively and to evaluate DMT-related effects longitudinally [ 28 , 38 ]. Conclusions Plasma biomarkers provide critical evidence to advance our understanding of APP variant-associated EOAD. Both APP variant-associated EOAD and sporadic EOAD showed a similar biomarker profile, with higher plasma levels of P-tau181, P-tau217, and GFAP than in AMC and NC. In contrast, the two groups exhibited divergent patterns in Aβ40 levels and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio. In addition, given the current sample size, APOE ε4 status did not modify the effects of the APP variant on plasma biomarkers among APP variant carriers.
