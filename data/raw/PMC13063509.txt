Complementary effect of a combined exercise and dietary supplement intervention in individuals with knee osteoarthritis: study protocol for a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial

Background Osteoarthritis is a common joint disease, especially among individuals aged 50 and older, leading to symptoms such as pain, stiffness, and functional limitations, particularly in weight-bearing joints like the knees. Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is increasingly prevalent, making effective treatment strategies critical. Exercise and dietary supplements are commonly used to manage symptoms, but the combination of these two approaches has not been widely studied. This study aims to evaluate the effects of combining exercise with dietary supplements on pain, physical function, and quality of life in individuals with KOA. Methods This trial will involve KOA patients, aged 55 and older. Participants will be assigned to one of three groups: (1) dietary supplement, (2) exercise with dietary supplement, (3) exercise with placebo dietary supplement. Randomization and double-blinding apply only to the dietary supplement in the exercise groups, while the supplement-only group serves as a non-randomized group. The exercise regimen will consist of resistance training, conducted under supervision. The dietary supplement will be a combination of glucosamine and collagen. The primary outcomes include pain levels and physical function, which will be assessed through questionnaires (WOMAC, SF-36), numeric rating scale, and various physical function tests (30 s chair stand test, 40-m fast-paced walk, timed up and go test, star climbing test, 6-min walk test, static balance, and maximal isometric voluntary contraction of knee flexor and extensor). Data will be collected at baseline (week 0), after completion of the 12-week intervention (week 13), and at 6 weeks post intervention (week 18). Discussion This study seeks to provide valuable insights into the combined effects of exercise and dietary supplements in managing KOA. It will fill the gap in current research by exploring whether the addition of a dietary supplement to an exercise regimen provides additional benefits. The findings could have important implications for clinical practices, offering a potential, non-invasive treatment strategy for improving pain management, physical function, and quality of life in individuals with KOA. Introduction Background and rationale {6a} Osteoarthritis is one of the most common joint diseases [ 1 ], primarily affecting individuals over the age of 50 [ 2 ] characterized by joint tissue abnormalities, cartilage degradation, and other changes [ 1 , 3 ], leading to pain and restricted daily movement [ 1 ], particularly in weight-bearing large joints like hips and knees [ 2 ]. The global prevalence of knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is increasing with age [ 4 ], necessitating exploration of effective treatment approaches. Current non-surgical management strategies prioritize physical exercise and weight management, followed by the use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) [ 5 , 6 ]. Exercise is a widely researched treatment for KOA. Resistance exercise improves symptoms, function, and muscle strength [ 7 ]. Similarly effective are indoor cycling, yoga, and water-based exercise [ 8 ], which are short-term effective in pain relief [ 9 , 10 ]. Meta-analyses show benefits of balance, stretching, different type of strength training, including home-based and supervised exercise [ 6 , 7 , 11 – 13 ]. Exercise improves balance, reduces fall risk [ 14 ], enhance walking function and postural control, alleviates pain and improves mobility [ 15 – 17 ]. Studies show exercise programs help with daily tasks in KOA patients [ 16 – 18 ] as well as the effects of dietary interventions in terms of weight reduction or dietary supplement intake [ 19 , 20 ]. Consumption of dietary supplements, such as glucosamine, is common in KOA treatment, though the clinical benefits remain inconclusive [ 21 ] with conflicting evidence regarding their effectiveness and recommendations [ 6 ]. Glucosamine reduces pain compared to placebo in KOA [ 22 ]. Curcumin improves pain and function significantly compared to placebo, but not to NSAIDs, while ginger shows clinical effects only on pain reduction [ 23 ]. Glucosamine and chondroitin have statistically significant but clinically questionable long-term benefits [ 24 ]. A meta-analysis suggests only pharmaceutical-grade chondroitin sulfate as a basic OA treatment [ 25 ] with moderate benefit on pain and function but with considerable heterogeneity among results [ 26 ]. Collagen reduces pain, stiffness and improves function [ 27 ]. Compared to glucosamine, collagen has demonstrated greater efficacy in alleviating symptoms [ 28 ]. Although the Osteoarthritis Research Society International (OARSI) conditionally opposes the use of collagen, and the other five global organizations included in the review of the latest recommendations regarding dietary supplements do not provide a stance on collagen due to insufficient evidence [ 29 ], collagen derivatives are gaining increasing attention [ 30 – 32 ]. Clinical studies reveal potentially beneficial effects of collagen [ 28 , 33 ]. While exercise has strong evidence for improving symptoms and function, the evidence supporting dietary supplements is less convincing. Although guidelines exist for physical activity and dietary approaches to manage KOA, these interventions are often studied separately. Despite numerous studies, there is a notable lack of trials examining the combined effects of exercise and dietary supplements [ 34 ], leaving questions about optimal approaches unanswered. The main purpose of the study is to assess the impact of combining exercise and dietary supplements on physical performance, pain, and quality of life in KOA patients. Objectives {7} The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of a combined intervention of dietary supplementation and exercise in the management of KOA. The study aims to address the following research questions: Does the addition of a dietary supplement to an exercise regimen provide an additive effect on the management of KOA? Does the incorporation of exercise alongside dietary supplementation yield greater therapeutic benefits in managing KOA? We hypothesize that participants receiving the combined intervention of dietary supplementation and exercise will experience a more substantial reduction in pain, enhanced physical function, and improved quality of life, compared to those receiving either exercise combined with a placebo or dietary supplementation alone. Trial design {8} The study is a parallel-group trial including a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled component. Participants will be allocated to one of three groups: (1) dietary supplements alone, (2) dietary supplements combined with exercise, or (3) placebo dietary supplement combined with exercise. In the two exercise groups, participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either the dietary supplement or placebo in a double-blind manner, while the supplement-only group represents a non-randomized group. The trial follows a superiority framework to evaluate whether the combined intervention of dietary supplements and exercise is more effective than either intervention alone. Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes Study setting {9} Participant recruitment and all intervention procedures will take place at the Community Healthcare Center dr. Adolf Drolc (Maribor, Slovenia) based on the physicians’ recommendation. Patients who seek medical care for KOA and meet the inclusion and exclusion criteria will be referred to a responsible researcher. The trial will be conducted in collaboration with the University of Primorska, Faculty of Health Sciences (Izola, Slovenia), which provides academic and methodological support. All exclusion criteria are predefined in the study protocol and will be assessed by the responsible researcher in collaboration with the physicians, based on documented medical records, radiological evaluations, and patient-reported history, ensuring standardized and objective decision-making. Who will take informed consent? {26a} The kinesiologist will provide a detailed explanation of the study procedures, risks, and benefits, and participants will be required to sign a written informed consent form before the testing begins. Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b} Participants will provide blood samples as part of the study to evaluate biomarkers relevant to the research objectives. These samples will be collected, processed, and analyzed according to the study protocol. Blood samples will be anonymized and stored securely. These samples will not be used for any additional or ancillary analyses beyond the scope of the current study. Interventions Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b} In this study, three comparator groups were selected in accordance with the study objectives. The design includes a parallel-group structure, with randomization and double-blinding applied only to the dietary supplement within the exercise intervention. Participants will be allocated to one of three groups: Dietary supplements alone Dietary supplements combined with exercise Placebo dietary supplement combined with exercise The choice of these comparator groups was made to assess the effects of dietary supplements and their combination with exercise on KOA symptoms. The inclusion of a placebo group aims to determine whether the effects observed in the other two groups exceed the effects of exercise alone or placebo. Intervention description {11a} Group 1 is the dietary supplement group. Participants will take a combined dietary supplement daily for 12 weeks according to the recommended doses. Specifically, participants will take one capsule of glucosamine sulfate (105 mg) with a meal and a glass of water three times daily, and each morning, one packet containing five sources of hydrolyzed collagen (9145 mg) with vitamin C (50 mg) and curcumin (250 mg), dissolved in a glass of water. While high-quality evidence for multi-ingredient supplements is limited, emerging studies suggest potential additive or synergistic effects. Combinations of collagen, glucosamine, and curcumin may improve symptoms and support cartilage more than single ingredients [ 35 , 36 ]. The chosen formulation reflects current evidence and common real-world use. Group 2 is exercise and dietary supplement group. A 12-week supervised exercise program will be implemented, consisting of approximately 60-min sessions conducted twice weekly (at least two rest days between sessions) for a total of 24 sessions. Sessions will take place in the gym of the Community Healthcare Center dr. Adolf Drolc Maribor and it will be offered in both morning and afternoon time slots to accommodate participant schedules. The program includes localized exercises targeting strength, flexibility, and balance, and complex exercises for the lower limbs and trunk. Aerobic exercises during warm-up will last 10–15 min, followed by strength exercises performed in circuit training format. Circuit exercises will include two series of 30–40 s of work and 20–30 s of rest. Balance exercises will be performed in 2–4 series of 20–60 s. Table 1 displays the exercises of the main training session (strength and balance exercises), with descriptions and progressions of the exercises shown. Flexibility exercises will be incorporated into the warm-up (dynamic stretching) and cool-down period (static stretching), with 2–3 repetitions of 30–60 s. Exercises will be gradually intensified based on current rehabilitation and prevention guidelines, using perceived exertion (0–10 scale), where an exertion rating of 7 corresponds to 70% 1RM [ 37 ]. Pain will be monitored using a Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) before exercise, and adjustments (e.g., shorter lever arm, lighter load, no load, smaller range of motion, load shift from the hip or ankle joint, relieving the knee joint by transferring body weight to the arms) will be made if pain increases by more than 2 points. In addition to the exercise program, participants will take the dietary supplement as described for Group 1. All participants will receive adherence reminders every two weeks to support regular intake of the dietary supplements during the 12-week intervention. For the exercise groups, reminders will be delivered in person during supervised sessions, while the supplement-only group will receive SMS reminders. All groups will undergo a brief 10-min educational session at the start of the study. This session will cover essential information about KOA, including its key risk factors and symptoms, available conservative treatment options, and instructions for using only paracetamol (rescue medication) for pain relief during the intervention if necessary. Participants will also be advised to maintain their usual dietary habits throughout the intervention period. The exercise group will also receive guidance on group training procedures (including visual demonstrations of exercises, organizational structure, adaptations for increased pain, rest periods between exercises and sets). Participants will receive detailed instructions on proper dietary supplement/placebo usage. Compliance with the interventions will be monitored through attendance records and self-reported adherence logs. Additionally, participants will record daily entries in their logs, noting pain levels on the NRS at rest and during movement, use of rescue medication, and any notable events during the day. All interventions will be conducted in small groups of up to 12 participants from March 2024 to May 2026. Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b} Modifications to the allocated intervention during the study will not be possible. However, if there is a severe deterioration in the participant’s condition or if the participant requests to withdraw from the study, they may discontinue participation. This will be clearly stated in the informed consent form, which the participants will sign upon entering the study. The participant will also be informed of their right to withdraw at any point without any negative consequences. Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c} To monitor adherence to the intervention protocols, participants will be educated about the importance of adherence to the protocols and their impact on the study outcomes. Participation in the exercise program will be documented. Participants will return any unused supplements each month (receiving a new box every month). They will also keep diaries of dietary supplement intake. Regular personal contacts will be made with participants, and the passive group will be contacted by phone. Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d} During the intervention, the use of rescue medication containing paracetamol will be permitted if necessary for pain relief, while other pain relief medications (NSAIDs) or the intake of additional dietary supplements will not be permitted during the study. The time and dosage of the rescue medication will be recorded in the participant's diary. Any other necessary medications unrelated to KOA will be permitted. Any therapies or interventions specific to the research question are prohibited to avoid potential bias in the results (e.g. physical therapy, intra-articular injections for symptom relief). Provisions for post-trial care {30} The likelihood of adverse effects is minimal. In the case of positive effects from the dietary supplements, participants in the placebo group will receive dietary supplement (as in Groups 1 and 2) for the duration of the intervention period (12 week) after the study concludes. Participants in the dietary supplement group will have the opportunity to join an identical exercise program, as received by groups 2 and 3, after the conclusion of the study. The research staff will provide information about the options for further care and support programs available after the conclusion of the study. All participants will receive their results and guidelines for exercising at home. Outcomes {12} The test battery will be conducted over two visits, performed before and after the 12-week intervention. Measurements during the first visit will be conducted in a group setting, followed by a second visit, conducted on an individual basis. Primary we will assess physical function, pain, and quality of life using self-assessment tools, questionnaires Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) and Short Form Health Survey (SF-36), and pain intensity on NRS before the measurements and after each task. Further we will assess the performance of daily activities using the 40-m fast-paced walk test (40mFPWT), handgrip strength (HGT), Stair Climb test (SCT) and the Senior Fitness Test Battery, which includes the 30-s Chair Stand Test (CST), Arm Curl Test (ACT), Sit and Reach Test (SRT), Back Scratch Test (BST), Timed up-and-go test (TUG), and the 6-min walk test (6MWT). At the end of the first visit, an activPAL sensor will be placed on the middle of the participant's right thigh for seven days. During the second visit, scheduled seven days after the initial assessment, participants will return the activPAL sensors and complete the Daily Activity Behaviours Questionnaire (DABQ). Knee mobility measurement, static balance task performance, and assessment of maximal isometric voluntary contractions (MVC) of knee flexors and extensors using a knee dynamometer will follow. Between both visits, we will collect blood samples as a secondary measurement to assess inflammatory markers, including CRP, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β. Additionally, participants will record their 3-day dietary intake to monitor nutritional patterns during this period. WOMAC, CST, 40mFPW, SCT and TUG will also be used in follow-up assessments conducted 6 weeks after the completion of the intervention. Participant timeline {13} Sample size {14} Based on findings from previous studies regarding changes in pain perception and distance walked in the 6MWT [ 38 , 39 ], the sample size was calculated using the G*Power 3.1 software (University of Düsseldorf, Germany) with an effect size of 0.3, α = 0.05, and a statistical power of 0.80 (β = 0.20). The required sample size per group is 28 (total of 84). Considering a 10% dropout rate while maintaining statistical power, the calculated sample size for each group is 31 (total of 93). Recruitment {15} Participant recruitment will take place at the Community Healthcare Center dr. Adolf Drolc in Maribor, based on the recommendation of physicians. To ensure adequate participant enrolment and achieve the target sample size, we will maintain regular communication with physicians, provide educational presentations at meetings, and organize personal visits to the 55 clinics involved in the study. Additionally, we will actively promote the study to potential participants by distributing informative materials and providing support to healthcare providers in explaining the study's benefits. Assignment of interventions: allocation Sequence generation {16a} Following the initial assessments, participants will be allocated to one of three study groups. Participants who enter the exercise arm will be further randomized in a 1:1 ratio to one of two treatment conditions: exercise with dietary supplement or exercise with placebo. The allocation sequence for the exercise groups will be generated by an independent statistician using computer-generated block randomization (randomly alternating blocks of 3 and 6) to ensure balanced group sizes. Stratification will be performed based on sex, age, and BMI. The Clinical Trial Randomization Tool [ 40 ] will be used to generate the sequence. Concealment mechanism {16b} Allocation concealment will apply to the randomization within the exercise group. The allocation sequence will be concealed using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes, ensuring that the allocation remains hidden until the moment of intervention assignment. The sequence will be created using a specialized randomization software designed for clinical trials. Implementation {16c} Participant enrollment will be conducted by physicians, who, in collaboration with the kinesiologist, will be responsible for recruiting and screening participants according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. After the kinesiologist explanation of the study details, participants will be informed about the option to choose between the exercise group or the non-exercise group. Those who choose the exercise group will then be randomly assigned to one of the two treatment conditions (exercise with dietary supplement or exercise with placebo). The kinesiologist will carry out the assignment of participants to the intervention groups, ensuring adherence to the randomization and allocation concealment plan. Assignment of interventions: blinding Who will be blinded {17a} After assignment to intervention groups, blinding will apply only to the dietary supplement in the exercise groups. Participants in these groups and personnel assessing outcomes will be blinded to supplement allocation. Participants will be assigned codes that do not reveal they receive the dietary supplement or placebo. This coding system will ensure that neither the participants, nor investigators can determine the supplement allocation. Participants in non-exercise, supplement-only group will be aware that they are receiving the active supplement and will not be blinded. Exercise itself cannot be blinded. Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b} Unblinding is permissible in the case of serious adverse effects or health complications that require immediate action to protect the health and safety of participants. The principal investigator will be responsible for unblinding. The procedure will include informing the participant about their assigned treatment, along with appropriate information and instructions for further care. Data collection and management Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a} Data collection will occur at baseline, after the intervention, and at follow-up points to monitor changes in the participants' condition. We will collect data related to primary and secondary outcomes. To promote data quality, we will ensure that assessor will be appropriately trained in the use of study instruments and protocols. Data collection will be supervised to ensure consistency. WOMAC is a self-assessment questionnaire consisting of 24 items, addressing pain, stiffness, and physical function in individuals with knee or hip osteoarthritis. Higher scores or a higher total sum (range: 0–96) indicate worse condition [ 41 ]. SF-36 is a self-assessment questionnaire that measures quality of life across of 36 items, grouped into eight health domains [ 42 ]. The SF-36 is the most commonly used generic measure of outcome used in osteoarthritis [ 43 ]. The Slovenian translation of the SF-36 is a reliable and valuable tool [ 44 ]. The WOMAC is the most commonly used disease-specific measure of outcome used in OA and it is more sensitive tool for assessing knee impairments compared to the SF-36 [ 45 , 46 ]. These outcomes are clinically relevant because they assess both the physical symptoms (pain, stiffness, function) and the quality of life of participants, which are key factors in managing KOA. The NRS is used to measure pain intensity on an 11-point scale. Participants will subjectively assess their current level of knee pain on a scale from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst possible pain). On average, a reduction of 2 points or a reduction of approximately 30% in the NRS represented a clinically important difference [ 47 ]. The CSR, 40mFPWT, TUG, 6MWT, and SCT are validated and reliable functional assessments commonly used to evaluate mobility, balance, walking speed, endurance, and lower extremity strength in individuals with KOA. All instruments have demonstrated reliability and validity in previous studies assessing KOA [ 48 ]. These tests are well-established in the literature for their reproducibility and sensitivity to changes in physical function, making them suitable for tracking the progression of physical abilities over time in clinical trials [ 49 ]. The CST, 40mFPW, and SCT are recommended by the OARSI as a minimal core performance-based tests in osteoarthritis research and clinical practice [ 48 ]. Therefore, in addition to the TUG and the WOMAC questionnaire, these tests will also be used in follow-up assessments conducted 6 weeks after the completion of the intervention. The CST, TUG, and 6MWT are also part of the Senior Fitness Test Battery, which includes additional tests such as the ACT, SRT, and BST [ 50 ]. These tests are widely used methods for a comprehensive assessment of physical function in the elderly population, making them appropriate for evaluation in this study. The activPAL3™ micro (PAL Technologies Ltd., Glasgow, Scotland) is a lightweight physical activity monitor (9 g; 55 × 25 × 5 mm) that quantifies postural allocation. When placed on the participant's anterior thigh, it directly measures thigh inclination and accurately identifies periods of sitting/lying, standing, and stepping [ 51 ]. The activPAL monitor is a valid and reliable measure of walking [ 52 ]. The DABQ is a valid and reliable tool and assesses the time spent sleeping, awake/sedentary, and engaging in physical activity over a seven-day period within a 24-h time frame [ 53 ]. Static balance will be assessed using a piezoelectric force plate (Kistler, Winterthur, Switzerland, model 9260AA) to measure forces on the surface and record the movement of the center of pressure (COP) over time. The test will be performed in two conditions: single-leg stance (eyes open) and parallel stance (eyes open and eyes closed). For each condition, three 30-s repetitions will be performed. Key measures include [ 54 ]: 1) duration of single-leg and parallel stances, 2) amplitude (overall and direction-specific) of COP movement, 3) velocity (overall and direction-specific) of COP movement, 4) frequency (overall and direction-specific) of COP movement. Good or excellent reliability for the assessment in older adults has already been reported for the single-leg stance test [ 55 ]. MVC of knee flexion and extension will be measured using a knee dynamometer (S2P, Science in Practice, Ltd., Ljubljana, Slovenia). The test will be performed under static conditions with a knee joint angle of 60° and hip flexion at 90° while seated. The participant will press against the device after a warm-up of two submaximal repetitions. This will be followed by three muscle contractions held for 3 s each at maximum force, with verbal encouragement to reach peak strength. The combination of these outcomes will help evaluate the intervention's effect on various aspects of physical function and quality of life, as well as functional recovery and the ability to perform daily activities. Data collection forms and detailed protocols for each assessment will be stored in the trial database. Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b} To ensure participant retention and minimize dropouts during follow-up, the following strategies will be implemented: Participants will receive detailed information at the beginning of the study about its purpose, significance, and anticipated benefits. Regular reminders will be provided every two weeks (in person during supervised sessions for the exercise groups, and via SMS for the supplement-only group). Monthly visits will allow participants to collect supplements, report unused doses, and address questions, supporting adherence and retention. Group exercise sessions will foster social interactions and mutual support, strengthening commitment to the program. Participants will have the option to make up missed exercise sessions on the same day (morning or afternoon sessions). Study staff will offer individualized support to help participants overcome challenges during the intervention. For participants who discontinue the intervention or deviate from the protocol (e.g., fail to complete exercise sessions or follow the supplement regimen), the following baseline data will still be collected: Demographic, health, and functional data collected at the start of the study. Outcome data: Key outcomes, such as pain levels and quality-of-life indicators, will be recorded at follow-up points, if participants consent. Compliance data: The number and reasons for missed exercise sessions or supplement doses. Participants who successfully complete all phases of the study will receive a symbolic token of appreciation (a home exercise program) as recognition for their effort. Attendance logs will be maintained for all exercise sessions. A compliance diary will track the intake of dietary supplements. Reasons for withdrawal or non-compliance will be documented and analyzed to improve future protocols. Data management {19} All data collected during the study will be entered into an electronic database. Data will be manually entered using double data entry to reduce the chance of errors. Participants will be assigned unique identification codes to ensure anonymity and protection of personal information. All sensitive data will be coded and stored separately from personal identifying information. Electronic data will be protected by passwords, with access restricted to authorized personnel. Physical data (e.g., paper questionnaires) will be stored in a locked cabinet at the research center. All data management procedures will comply with relevant data protection laws to ensure the integrity and accuracy of the data throughout the study. Confidentiality {27} Personal data of potential and enrolled participants will be collected and handled in accordance with the strictest standards of privacy and confidentiality protection. Personal data of participants will be collected at the beginning of the study and stored in an anonymous form, where each participant will receive a unique identification number. All personal data will be stored in secure systems with appropriate security measures such as encryption and password protection. Personal data will be retained only for as long as necessary to fulfill legal obligations and the study's objectives. Anonymized data will be made available to interested researchers using a publicly accessible repository. Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33} Blood samples will be collected from participants at predetermined time points during the study. The sampling will be carried out by trained medical personnel, adhering to the highest standards to ensure the safety and comfort of the participants. The blood samples will be analyzed for molecular parameters relevant to the current research. These analyses will be conducted by certified laboratories using validated methods to ensure accuracy and reliability of the results. After collection, the blood samples will be properly labeled with anonymous identifiers to protect the participants' privacy. The samples will be stored in specially designed cooling devices within secure facilities accessible only to authorized personnel. Samples that are no longer needed will be disposed of in a safe and environmentally friendly manner, in compliance with legal regulations, and will not be used for other studies. Statistical methods Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a} Data from questionnaires and functional test results will be entered into a database and subsequently transferred into SPSS for reporting and statistical analysis. All statistical analyses will follow an intention-to-treat approach [ 56 ]. The investigator conducting the analyses will remain blinded to the treatment allocation. Analyses will be performed using SPSS 22 (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize participant characteristics (age, sex, BMI) and baseline values of outcome measures. Continuous variables will be reported as means, standard deviations, and ranges. The differences between groups at baseline will be tested. In the case of significant differences, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) will be applied, with the baseline values as the covariate. To analyze the primary and secondary outcomes of this study, the normality of distribution for all variables will be assessed (Shapiro–Wilk test and Mauchly's test of sphericity; p &lt; 0.05). Non-normal distributed variables will be appropriately transformed before further analyses. Differences of the functional outcome variables between baseline and after intervention between groups will be tested using a two-way repeated measures analysis of variance and paired two-tailed post-hoc t-tests with Bonferroni correction. The primary comparison will be addressed using a 2 × 2 two-way analysis of variance (the first research question will be addressed using a two-way analysis of variance, including groups 2 and 3; the second research question will be addressed using a two-way analysis of variance, including groups 1 and 2). Significant differences will be considered at the threshold of p &lt; 0.05. Additionally, effect size will be calculated and interpreted as small (0–0.2), medium (0.2–0.6), large (0.6–1.2), or very large (&gt; 1.2) [ 57 ]. Interim analyses {21b} There will be no interim analyses. The decision to continue or potentially terminate the study will be based on the occurrence of serious adverse events directly related to the intervention. Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses) {20b} Subgroup analyses will be performed to assess whether the effects of the intervention differ based on specific participant characteristics, such as age, baseline severity of osteoarthritis, and number of exercise sessions attended. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the robustness of the findings. These analyses will explore whether the results hold true when excluding participants with missing data or outliers, or when applying alternative statistical methods. Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c} All statistical analyses will follow an intention-to-treat approach [ 56 ], including all participants as originally randomized, regardless of adherence. To handle missing data, we will apply multiple imputation [ 58 ]. In addition to the intention-to-treat analysis, a sensitivity (per-protocol) analysis including participants with ≥ 80% adherence to both the exercise intervention and supplement intake will be performed. Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant level-data and statistical code {31c} The full protocol will be made publicly available as part of the published study manuscript. Participant-level data will not be shared publicly to ensure the privacy and confidentiality of participants. The statistical code used for data analysis will also not be made available. Oversight and monitoring Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee {5d} The principal investigator will oversee the study and provide overall direction. The coordinating team will manage daily tasks, including recruiting participants, collecting data, and maintaining records. They will meet weekly to monitor progress. The trial steering committee will provide oversight and guidance for the study, meeting quarterly to review progress and ensure safety. The data management team will handle data collection, storage, and analysis, meeting as needed to address any issues. Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a} Due to the nature of the study, where the planned interventions are low-risk and in accordance with existing clinical guidelines, as well as the limited number of serious adverse events reported in previous studies, it is assessed that the risk to participants is minimal. In light of this, and considering the established mechanisms for monitoring participant safety throughout the study, the establishment of a separate data monitoring committee is not deemed necessary. Adverse event reporting and harms {22} All adverse events will be collected from participants throughout the trial period. Participants will be encouraged to report any health changes immediately. Study staff will also monitor for any adverse events during scheduled visits. Each adverse event will be assessed for severity, causality, and relatedness to the trial intervention. Adverse events will be documented and reported according to regulatory requirements. All adverse events will be included in periodic safety reports. Participants experiencing adverse events will receive appropriate medical care, and the trial will be adjusted as necessary to ensure participant safety. In the case of serious adverse events, the principal investigator and the research team will review the event to assess the need for trial modifications or termination. Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23} This trial does not include specific auditing procedures, as all processes will be carried out in accordance with the protocol, ethical guidelines, and regulatory requirements. Compliance will be ensured through regular monitoring by the research team, including periodic reviews of participant data, safety assessments, and protocol adherence. Any deviations from the protocol or safety concerns will be promptly addressed within the research team and reported to the appropriate authorities. While there will not be a formal independent auditing process, the research team will ensure transparency and adherence to regulatory and ethical standards. Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g. trial participants, ethical committees) {25} Any significant changes to the protocol, such as modifications to eligibility criteria, outcomes, or analysis methods, will be promptly communicated to all relevant parties. If the changes directly affect participants, they will be informed through written notifications and, if necessary, follow-up discussions with the study team to ensure understanding and consent. All protocol amendments will be submitted to the relevant ethical committees for review and approval before implementation. The study team will be promptly informed of protocol changes through meetings to ensure proper understanding and implementation. If the changes are significant enough to affect the trial registration details, the registry will be updated accordingly. If the changes impact the published trial findings, they will be disclosed in subsequent publications to ensure transparency. Dissemination plans {31a} The plan for communicating trial results to participants, healthcare professionals, the public, and other relevant groups will include the following strategies: Publication of results: The results will be published in peer-reviewed scientific journals after data analysis is completed. Communication to participants: Participants will be informed of the trial outcomes through direct communication, including a summary of the findings once the trial is completed. Public communication: Trial results may be shared with the public via press releases, institutional websites, or other public forums to ensure broader access to information. Healthcare professionals: Results will be communicated to healthcare professionals, including those involved in participant recruitment, as well as others who regularly care for individuals with KOA, such as medical doctor, physiotherapists, kinesiologists, specialists in physical medicine and rehabilitation, orthopedic specialists, dietitians, nurses, and other interested parties. This aims to ensure that the findings can directly inform clinical practice and patient care. Discussion The purpose of this trial is to evaluate the effectiveness of a combined intervention of exercise and dietary supplementation in managing KOA. In the exercise groups, participants will be randomized to receive either the dietary supplement or placebo in a double-blind manner. This study aims to determine whether adding dietary supplementation to an exercise program provides an additional therapeutic benefit compared to exercise with placebo supplementation or dietary supplementation alone (in the non-randomized supplement only group). Unlike many previous studies that have focused on either exercise or dietary supplementation independently [ 6 – 8 ], this trial explores the additional effects of both interventions. The exercise program is designed to target mainly strength, while dietary supplementation will focus on commonly used supplements such as glucosamine and collagen, which have shown mixed results in KOA treatment [ 21 , 22 , 27 ]. This trial will provide valuable insights into whether their combination leads to greater improvements in physical performance, pain management, and quality of life for individuals with KOA. The structured and supervised nature of the exercise program ensures consistency and reliability, while most exercises require minimal equipment, making them feasible for home-based implementation. The inclusion of dietary supplements offers an easily integrated complementary approach to enhance potential treatment effects. Additionally, a 6-week follow-up allows for short-term effect assessment and provides insight into the sustainability of intervention outcomes. A key strength of this study is its comprehensive approach, combining exercise and dietary supplementation to address multiple aspects of KOA management. The chosen combination of supplements reflects common real-world multi-component use, enhancing the practical relevance and applicability of the findings. The well-defined inclusion criteria help minimize variability in the sample, enhancing internal validity. Conducting the study in a real-world setting increases the relevance and applicability of findings to the broader KOA population. However, certain limitations should be acknowledged. A small sample size may limit the generalizability of the results. The inclusion criteria, while strengthening internal validity, may restrict findings to specific subgroups, thereby limiting broader applicability. The absence of participant blinding in the supplement-only group represents a potential source of bias, although outcome assessors and data analysts remained blinded. Potential non-compliance with the exercise program or dietary supplement regimen could impact reliability. Additionally, the 6-week follow-up may not capture long-term effects, and the study does not assess the prolonged impact of exercise and dietary supplements on disease progression or sustained functional improvement. Furthermore, variability in supplement effects and potential interactions with medications may introduce heterogeneity in the findings. Despite these limitations, this study aims to address gaps in the current literature and provide evidence-based insights into the combined impact of exercise and dietary supplementation for KOA management. The findings will contribute to clinical practice, guiding healthcare professionals in offering more effective and holistic treatment options. Results will be disseminated upon study completion.
