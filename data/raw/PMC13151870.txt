Tongxinluo capsule in the treatment of cerebral small vessel disease: protocol of a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre clinical study (TOPS-CSVD)

Abstract Background Tongxinluo capsule has demonstrated multiple beneficial effects on microcirculation, endothelial cells and inflammation. However, evidence supporting their efficacy in improving vascular cognitive decline is limited. This trial aims to investigate the potential benefits of Tongxinluo capsules in Chinese patients with cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) who have been diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment. Design Thistrail is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre study involving 1052 individuals aged 50–80 years with MRI-confirmed CSVD and a diagnosis of mild cognitive impairment. Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either Tongxinluo capsules or placebo. The treatment period spans 12 months, with follow-up assessments conducted every 3 months to collect cognitive scale results and other relevant data. Study outcome The primary outcome of this clinical trial is the change in the Vascular Dementia Assessment Scale-cognitive subscale from baseline to 12 months. The primary safety outcome focuses on the incidence of adverse vital signs, including abnormalities in body temperature, heart rate, respiration and blood pressure. Discussion This trial has the potential to offer novel insights into the management of cognitive decline associated with CSVD through the application of traditional Chinese medicine. Trial registration number NCT06061692 .

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC Tongxinluo capsules have demonstrated favourable safety and efficacy in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction primarily through their protective effects on microvascular endothelial cells. WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS This study explores the safety and efficacy of Tongxinluo capsules in Chinese patients with mild cognitive impairment related to cerebral small vessel diseases. Additionally, it explores neuroimaging changes following treatment to discuss the potential mechanisms underlying the effects of Tongxinluo capsules. HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY This study advances our understanding of managing cognitive outcomes in cerebral small vessel disease and highlights the necessity for high-quality clinical trials and mechanistic research in traditional Chinese medicine. These efforts have the potential to broaden treatment options and facilitate earlier preventive strategies for vascular cognitive impairment. Introduction and rationale Cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) is a common neurological condition caused by various pathological processes affecting the brain’s small arteries, arterioles, capillaries and small veins. It represents the most prevalent underlying pathology of vascular cognitive impairment (VCI) and is independently associated with global cognitive decline. 1 Despite controlling vascular risk factors remains a critical strategy for treating CSVD, there is no reliable treatment that effectively improves cognitive outcomes. The clinical diagnosis of CSVD relies on multimodal brain MRI imaging, which typically reveals characteristic features such as white matter hyperintensities, lacunes, microbleeds and enlarged perivascular spaces. 2 4 Among patients with CSVD, a small proportion may present with symptomatic lacunar infarcts, while a larger number experience a gradual decline in cognitive function along with the progression of imaging biomarkers. 5 In recent years, most randomised controlled trials (RCTs) on CSVD have primarily focused on evaluating the preventive effects of medications on lacunar stroke occurrence, with cognitive function often relegated to a secondary outcome. 6 7 These RCTs typically involve patients with clinically symptomatic lacunar infarcts, with limited representation of individuals exhibiting progressive cognitive impairment due to CSVD in the absence of stroke events. Cognitive decline in CSVD is a chronic and progressive process. In the early stages, the accumulation of vascular risk factors leads to reduced cerebral blood flow and perfusion, which subsequently induces endothelial dysfunction and compromises the integrity of blood-brain barrier (BBB). 8 This cascade of events initiates and accelerates neuronal damage and inflammatory responses triggered by hypoperfusion, ultimately contributing to cognitive decline. 9 Patients in the early stages of CSVD often exhibit cognitive decline that has not yet reached the diagnostic threshold for vascular dementia, a condition referred to as vascular mild cognitive impairment (VMCI). Early intervention during the VMCI phase holds promise for delaying or even reversing cognitive deterioration. However, there is currently no effective treatment specifically for VMCI. Given the significant mechanistic differences between VMCI and Alzheimer’s disease (AD)-related mild cognitive impairment (MCI), therapeutic strategies targeting microcirculation or the BBB may offer greater clinical potential. TraditionalChinese medicine, including herbal products, has gained increasing recognition for its clinical relevance in disease prevention, management and overall health improvement. 10 These modalities play a crucial role in enhancing health outcomes, particularly for chronic conditions where conventional treatments may have limitations. 10 Traditional Chinese medicine, encompassing herbal remedies and acupuncture, has been widely used for its multitarget and holistic approach to treatment. 11 12 Growing evidence supports its benefits in managing neurological disorders, cardiovascular diseases and metabolic conditions. 11 12 As interest in traditional Chinese medicine continues to rise, rigorous clinical research, including well-designed RCTs, is essential to validate its effectiveness and integrate it into contemporary healthcare practices. Tongxinluo (TXL) is a multifunctional Chinese herbal compound widely used in the treatment of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. 13 Its long-term clinical use has demonstrated good safety and tolerability. 14 15 A previous RCT showed that TXL, when used as an adjunctive therapy for patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, significantly reduces major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events. 16 In cases of ischaemic stroke, TXL has been reported to improve neurological deficits and reduce cerebral infarct size. 17 The recently published randomised clinical trial ‘Tongxinluo for Acute Ischemic Stroke’, demonstrated TXL’s significant efficacy in acute cerebrovascular disease treatment. 15 Compared with placebo plus standard care, 90-day treatment with TXL resulted in superior functional outcomes (the modified Rankin scale [mRS] 0–1: 65.8% vs 59.1%; OR=1.33, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.59, p=0.002) while maintaining comparable safety profiles (adverse events: p=0.95; serious adverse events: p=0.64). 15 These findings provide high-quality evidence supporting TXL’s clinical application in cerebrovascular disorders. The cerebral-protective effects of TXL are believed to be mediated primarily through reducing serum lipid levels and stabilising vulnerable plaques, enhancing the integrity of the BBB, attenuating neuronal apoptosis, promoting axonal plasticity and stimulating angiogenesis and neurogenesis in the peri-infarct area and subventricular zone so that they delay neuronal death and ameliorate clinical symptoms. 18 22 The pathogenesis of VMCI involves ischaemic cerebral injury, neuroinflammation and microcirculation dysfunction, all of which are potential therapeutic targets of TXL. 23 Based on these mechanistic insights, we hypothesise that TXL may have therapeutic efficacy for patients with CSVD-related VMCI and have designed the present study to investigate this potential. The trial is an investigator-initiated, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre clinical study designed to evaluate the efficacy of TXL capsules in the treatment of CSVD. It is a The primary outcome is the change of the Vascular Dementia Assessment Scale-cognitive subscale (VADAS-cog) after 1 year of treatment. This trial is the first RCT to evaluate the effects of TXL on vascular cognitive impairment. In this trial, we employed advanced multimodal imaging techniques—such as pseudo-continuous arterial spin labelling, MR angiography and diffusion tension imaging—to investigate the macrovascular and microvascular changes and elucidate the potential mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of TXL. Methods Design This is a 12-month, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre clinical study( figure 1 ). It includes a 12-month treatment period and four follow-up visits scheduled at 3-month intervals. Participants who meet the eligibility criteria will be randomly assigned to either the TXL group or the placebo (control) group through the Interactive Web Response System (IWRS). The study protocol adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials reporting guidelines to ensure transparency and rigour. 24 Figure 1 Study flowchart. Randomisation Study medicine numbers were generated via block randomisation using proprietary software, ensuring a 1:1 allocation ratio between the TXL and control groups. Subgroups were labelled and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio. Researchers independent of the study encoded the study drug (TXL capsule or placebo) based on these numbers. Randomisation and drug blinding were also managed through SAS software and imported into the IWRS. Following screening, the eligible investigators entered participants’ information into the IWRS, obtained the randomisation number (eg, 0001) and corresponding medicine number (eg, D0001), and dispensed the assigned treatment accordingly. Online urgent unblinding is permitted when necessary for managing adverse events as determined by the principal investigator, with the reasons documented in the IWRS. Intervention TXL capsules and placebo capsules are identical in colour, packaging, labelling, contents and shape. Eligible patients will be randomly assigned to receive either TXL capsules or placebo capsules (260 mg/capsule, four capsules, three times a day) for a duration of 12 months. To improve adherence, communication groups will be established to supervise trial procedures and essential medical care services will be provided to participants throughout the study. The study period begins with the initiation of medication and extends over the 12-month treatment phase. Concurrent medications must adhere to established clinical guidelines and be thoroughly documented. The primary prohibited drugs in this study include cholinesterase inhibitors (eg, donepezil, carbabatine, galantamine), non-competitive N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor antagonists (eg, memantine), buphthalide and Chinese patent medicines containing similar components to those in TXL capsules. Subgroup analysis Subgroup analyses will be conducted at advanced centres equipped with sophisticated multimodal brain MRI capabilities. From the 60 participating subcentres, 5–6 centres will be selected and all participants from these advanced centres will form the imaging subgroup (n=200). This subgroup will undergo comprehensive imaging assessments at baseline and at 12 months to evaluate changes following TXL administration. In the imaging subgroup, additional MRI sequences will be collected, including but not limited to three-dimensional T1, T2, fluid-attenuated inversion recovery, susceptibility weighted imaging, diffusion tensor imaging, pseudo-continuous arterial spin labelling, magnetic resonance angiography and functional MRI. These advanced imaging techniques hold significant potential for exploring the pathological substrates of VCI, such as microstructural changes, BBB dysfunction and recovery and changes in functional network connectivity. Furthermore, these techniques also provide valuable insights into the mechanisms by which TXL may ameliorate cognitive decline. Sample size calculation The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the change in VADAS-cog score from baseline after 1 year of treatment. The power analysis incorporated data from three pivotal clinical trials: the DL-3-n-butylphthalide trial in vascular cognitive impairment without dementia due to subcortical ischaemic small vessel disease (VD), the phase 3 lecanemab trial in early AD and the phase 3 sodium oligomannate (GV-971) trial in mild-to-moderate AD. 25 27 Based on these reference studies and assuming a 5% two-sided with an SD of 8.0 for the change in VADAS-cog from baseline after 12 months of treatment, a total of 894 patients (447 per group with a 1:1 ratio) were needed to achieve 80% power to detect a 1.5-point drug-placebo difference. Given an expected dropout rate of 15%, the adjusted sample size should be 1052 participants, with 526 patients in each group. At the time of manuscript submission, 86% of the target enrolment (n=900/1052) had been successfully recruited. Data Safety and Monitoring Board The Data Safety and Monitoring Board (DSMB), composed of academic members and independent statisticians, will regularly monitor the progress of this study to ensure compliance with the highest ethical standards and the safety of participants. Regular reports will be submitted to the committee chair following each DSMB meeting, detailing the study’s progress, safety data and any recommendations for modifications or continuation of the trial. Statistical analyses According to the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, the modified ITT set will include participants who were randomised and have at least one record of medication and efficacy evaluation. The per-protocol set will exclude patients from the modified ITT who have medication compliance of less than 80%, have dropped out or have experienced a major protocol violation; this set is designed for sensitivity analysis. The safety set is mainly used for safety analyses. Regarding baseline characteristics, for continuous variables, we will calculate the number of cases, mean, SD, median, minimum and maximum values. For count and ordinal variables, we will calculate frequency and percentage. Both descriptive and inferential statistical results (p values) will be provided. The primary outcome was the change in VADAS-cog score from the initial score after 1 year of treatment. A covariance model was employed to analyse the primary outcome between the TXL and control groups, with missing values imputed using the last observation carried forward method. In this model, the treatment group was treated as a fixed factor, while the baseline VADAS-cog score was included as a covariate. The difference between the adjusted average changes in VADAS-cog scores from the beginning of the study for the TXL and control groups after 1 year of treatment was calculated, along with its 95% CI. A mixed model for repeated measures was used for sensitivity analysis. The model used the baseline VADAS-cog score as a covariate and included the treatment group, evaluation time point and the interaction term between treatment group and evaluation time point as fixed effects. The difference between the adjusted average changes in VADAS-cog scores from the beginning of the study for the TXL and control groups after 1 year of treatment was calculated, along with its 95% CI. For secondary efficacy outcomes, including the changes from baseline in the MMSE, CDR, NPI-Q, ADL and gait assessments after treatment, descriptive statistics were performed. A covariance model was employed to analyse these outcomes between the TXL and control groups. Besides, for categorical indicators, such as the incidence of combined endpoints, the incidence of individual events of combined endpoints and CIBIC-Plus scores from interviews, the stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test will be used. A two-tailed p value of &lt;0.05 will be considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses will be performed by the SAS V.9.4 software. Data collection and management This study uses an electronic data collection (EDC) system to collect and manage research data, ensuring the traceability of clinical trial information. The data management process must adhere to Good Clinical Practice guidelines to maintain the authenticity, completeness and accuracy of the data. A researcher or research coordinator is required to enter and submit data according to the electronic case report form filling guide. Subsequently, the clinical research monitor, data administrator and medical staff will review the data, respectively. Any discrepancies or issues identified during the review will be flagged as queries within the EDC system and communicated to the researcher for prompt resolution. Project management The executive committee will oversee the trial’s progress and review available blinded data. Key decisions will be discussed during face-to-face meetings conducted by the executive committee, which will then provide further guidance and instructions to ensure the study’s alignment with its objectives and regulatory standards. Discussion VCI encompasses a spectrum of conditions, ranging from subjective cognitive decline and VMCI to vascular dementia. 28 The primary focus of managing VCI is to prevent the progression of VMCI to VD. The TOPS-CSVD study is multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy of TXL capsules in patients with MCI related to cerebral small VD. Participants will be administered either TXL or placebo and will undergo comprehensive cognitive assessments at 3-month intervals. By integrating cognitive scales with multimodal brain MRI imaging, including advanced MRI sequences, the study aims to evaluate the impact of TXL on VCI and explore the underlying mechanisms. The findings from the TOPS-CSVD study have the potential to provide a new treatment option for VCI, promote further research on cognitive improvement, influence clinical guidelines and advance earlier interventions. Due to the high heterogeneity in its pathogenesis, VCI is classified into several subtypes, including multi-infarct dementia, small vessel dementia, strategic infarct dementia, hypoperfusion dementia, haemorrhagic dementia, hereditary vascular dementia and mixed dementia. 8 Focusing on a specific subtype helps ensure the homogeneity of enrolled subjects and minimise the potential bias. In the TOPS-CSVD study, rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria have been implemented to eliminate patients with recent stroke, hereditary vascular cognitive impairment and other conditions (eg, inflammation, metabolic disorders, autoimmune diseases) that may affect cognition. By specifically targeting patients with CSVD, this trial could evaluate whether a specific defined patient subgroup could benefit from TXL treatment, thereby reducing the confounding effects of heterogeneity inherent in the broader VCI population. Based on the traditional Chinese medicine network (luo) theory, TXL has been approved as a representative treatment for cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Its safety and tolerability have been validated through long-term clinical use. 29 The network (luo) theory focuses on addressing microvascular pathology by protecting microvascular endothelial cells—a concept further supported by clinical trials demonstrating the efficacy of TXL capsules in patients with acute myocardial infarction and acute ischaemic stroke. Moreover, TXL has also demonstrated therapeutic benefits such as improving microcirculation perfusion, promoting the growth of brain microvascular cells, stabilising the BBB and reducing inflammation. 22 30 These mechanisms align with the potential therapeutic targets for CSVD-related cognitive impairment. Traditional Chinese medicine has often faced criticism, as clinical trials in this field are frequently limited by small sample sizes and reliance on surrogate endpoints. 30 31 Nonetheless, traditional Chinese medicine offers a holistic approach through comprehensive multitargeted interventions, which may provide unique advantages in the treatment and prevention of VCI compared with conventional western medicine. In contrast, our study addresses these limitations by incorporating a large sample size, an extended intervention period, and a rigorous design that includes randomisation, placebo control and blinding. As a result, this trial may serve as a benchmark for future high-quality clinical research evaluating traditional therapies aimed at cognitive improvement, bridging the gap between traditional Chinese medicine and modern evidence-based medicine. Compared with other RCT studies targeting CSVD, the TOPS-CSVD study offers several key advantages. First, the trial is conducted across 60 centres in diverse regions of China, enrolling over 1000 subjects and administering a 12-month treatment regimen. This large-scale, multicentre design enhances the reliability and generalisability of the findings. Second, the trial focuses on the changes in the VADAS-cog as the primary outcome. By incorporating assessments of motor function, executive function, daily living abilities and behavioural and psychological symptoms, it provides a more comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s clinical status. Third, neuroimaging analyses are performed at both baseline and follow-up, with advanced multimodal imaging employed in subgroups. This methodology deepens our understanding of the mechanisms by which TXL may improve cognitive decline. Collectively, these approaches facilitate a thorough analysis and validation of the efficacy and underlying mechanisms of TXL capsules. Several limitations of our trial warrant consideration. First, cognitive decline in patients with MCI may progress slowly over 1 year. However, our study not only recruits participants with MCI, but also those with moderate-to-severe cerebral small VD as evidenced by brain MRI. These participants are estimated to experience a faster rate of cognitive decline compared with patients with MCI. Additionally, the VADAS-cog we used in our study incorporates assessments of executive function and attention, in addition to the domains covered by the VADAS-cog, making it more sensitive for detecting changes in VMCI. Furthermore, based on a careful review of previous studies, we believe it is reasonable to anticipate a favourable drug-placebo difference following a 1-year treatment with TXL in patients with VMCI. 6 25 Second, in clinical practice, AD often coexists with VD, both contributing to accelerated cognitive decline. Pure VD accounts for only 5%–10% of dementia cases, with mixed dementia representing a significant proportion. 32 The pathological changes associated with CSVD may promote amyloid and tau deposition, making the coexistence of CSVD and AD more common in the elderly. 33 Consequently, we did not exclude subjects with mixed pathology during recruitment. In future analyses, we plan to assess hippocampal and temporal lobe volumes via cranial MRI and measure levels of phosphorylated tau (pTau217, 231) in peripheral blood to better elucidate the role of neurodegenerative factors in cognitive impairment. Conclusions The TOPS-CSVD study will offer reliable data on whether administration of TXL capsules is an effective and safe treatment strategy compared with placebo for the improvement of MCI in patients with cerebral small VD.
