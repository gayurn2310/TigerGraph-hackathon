Targeting the HDAC4-NHE6-endosomal pH axis restores amyloid-β clearance and cognitive function in Alzheimer's disease mice

Background Impaired clearance of amyloid-β (Aβ) is a major pathological hallmark of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Although histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitors show therapeutic potential, their clinical translation for AD is hampered by poor blood brain barrier (BBB) penetration and an incomplete understanding of their mechanism in Aβ clearance. Here, angiopep2-conjugated nanoparticles (SAHA@LIPO-ANG2) for efficient BBB translocation and delivery of the HDAC inhibitor vorinostat (SAHA) was developed and its underlying mechanisms were validated. Results Our result demonstrates that SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 potently inhibits HDAC4 nuclear translocation, which was identified as a key upstream event responsible for the transcriptional repression of sodium-hydrogen exchanger 6 (NHE6). Restoration of NHE6 expression rectifies endosomal hyperacidification, thereby rescuing the trafficking and plasma membrane expression of the Aβ clearance receptor, low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1). Furthermore, this HDAC4-NHE6-pH axis modulates the neuroimmune microenvironment to enhance Aβ clearance through multiple synergistic mechanisms: it upregulates phagocytic receptors and recruit microglial to phagocytize Aβ plaques, while concurrently reactivating autophagy-lysosomal function in astrocytes by increasing LAMP2 expression. Consequently, treatment with SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 in 5xFAD mice significantly reduced Aβ burden, suppressed neuroinflammation, rescued synaptic loss, and ultimately reversed cognitive deficits. Conclusions Our study not only elucidates a HDAC4-NHE6-pH regulatory axis in AD pathogenesis but also establishes a multifaceted nanotherapeutic strategy for restoring Aβ homeostasis. Our findings may provide therapeutic strategies for treating amyloid-related diseases. Introduction AD poses a growing public health challenge worldwide, affecting over 50 million people [ 1 ]. Characterized by the deposition of insoluble Aβ plaques, AD manifests as progressive memory impairment and cognitive decline [ 2 ]. Current therapeutic drugs, such as the HDAC inhibitor, are shown to reduce Aβ burden in AD mice brains [ 3 ]. However, poor blood brain barrier (BBB) penetration and unclear mechanism limit its clinical translation for AD. As a HDAC inhibitor, vorinostat is largely restricted to peripheral tissues, with minimal accumulation in the central nervous system (CNS) [ 4 ]. An estimated 40–65% of AD patients with the APOE ε4 [ 5 , 6 ], which has been confirmed with more significant nuclear HDAC4 accumulation [ 7 – 10 ]. HDAC4 nuclear translocation leads to neurodegeneration, cognitive dysfunction and reduced Aβ clearance [ 11 – 13 ], while repressing the transcription of multiple downstream genes [ 14 , 15 ]. Among sodium-hydrogen exchangers, NHE6 is highly expressed in the brain [ 16 ]. However, transcriptomic analyses of microarray data reported by Blalock et al. reveal that NHE6 expression is significantly reduced throughout neurodegeneration progression [ 16 ]. In prior study, inhibition of HDAC4 nuclear translocation upregulates NHE6 expression [ 17 , 18 ], linking NHE6 expression to Aβ aggregation have opened new therapeutic avenues for AD. NHE6 is an endosomal transmembrane protein highly expressed in axons and dendrites, playing a role in regulating synaptic development and plasticity [ 19 ], loss of endosomal NHE6 presents with widespread cortical and subcortical neuronal loss, gliosis, reduced lysosomal numbers, decreased protease activity, and impaired autophagic flux which are associated with neurodegeneration [ 16 , 20 , 21 ]. Disruption of lysosomal and autophagic function leads to the accumulation of unwanted, potentially neurotoxic proteins [ 22 ]. Besides, Aβ and the inflammatory environment can suppress the expression of autophagy-related genes in astrocytes [ 23 – 25 ]. These observations prompt us to consider the broader role of NHE6 in AD. Besides, NHE6 participates in the regulation of endosomal pH, endosomal trafficking and signalling. Reduced NHE6 is implicated in excessive endosomal acidification [ 19 , 26 ], influencing LRP1 recycling back to the cell surface which undergoes constitutive endocytosis from the cell membrane [ 27 ]. Cumulative evidence indicates that LRP1 participate in the pathogenesis of AD by regulating Aβ accumulation or neurodegenerative processes. As a ligand, LRP1 regulates Aβ metabolism and brain homeostasis in the brain, impairment of which likely contributes to AD patholohical development [ 28 – 31 ]. The involvement of LRP1 in regulating the pathogenic role of APOE ε4 have been demonstrated in several preclinical studies [ 32 ], indicating the importance of LRP1 in AD. Knockout of LRP1 increases proinflammatory cytokine production in microglia and astrocytes accompanied by abnormal activation of them [ 33 – 35 ], significantly enhancing Aβ induced neuroinflammation [ 36 – 38 ]. These data demonstrate that the LRP1 receptor mediates the clearance of Aβ [ 39 – 42 ]. Therefore, APOE ε4-induced nuclear translocation of HDAC4 suppresses NHE6 expression, leads to endosomal acidification, and ultimately impedes LRP1 trafficking to the membrane surface. Therefore, the primary aim of this study is to develop a non-invasive and biosafety nanoparticle delivery system for transporting vorinostat into the central nervous system and to validate the HDAC4 inhibitor-related mechanisms. Here, we show that the selective down-regulation of NHE6 in cells causes a pathological acidification of endosomal pH. This leads to endosomal sequestration and cell surface loss of the Aβ receptor, LRP1. Besides, we propose a therapeutic strategy in which using angiopep-2-conjugated nanoparticles (SAHA@LIPO-ANG2) to achieve high-efficiency blood-brain barrier penetration after intravenous injection [ 43 – 45 ]. HDAC inhibitor SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 enhances Aβ clearance through multiple pathways: firstly, it decreases nuclear translocation of HDAC4, restoring NHE6 expression, trafficking LRP1 to the cell surface. Additionally, it upregulates phagocytic receptors CD36 and recruit microglia to engulf Aβ plaques, while simultaneously reactivating the autophagic-lysosomal function of astrocytes. The public health challenges posed by AD compel us to seek novel approaches to alleviate or even improve the condition. Our results could have prognostic implications for patients with AD and provide therapeutic strategies for the treatment of amyloid-related diseases. Material synthesis and characterization Liposomes were prepared using the thin-film dispersion method. Specifically, Weigh Se−Se-PEG-Ang2, Cholesterol, DPPC, DOTAP in molar ratio 3:30:15:20 were weighed at a molar ratio of 1.5:38.5:50:10. The mixture was dissolved in 3 mL CHCl₃ after thorough shaking. The organic solvent was then evaporated using a rotary evaporator at 45 °C for 45 minutes to form a uniform thin lipid film on the inner wall of the round-bottom flask. Subsequently, nitrogen gas was purged for 2 minutes to completely dry the lipid membrane. After that, 20mg SAHA were dissolved in 200ul DMSO solution and added to the hydrated lipid film. The mixture was shaken at 45 °C with shaking for 30 minutes to form liposomal nanoparticles, designated as SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 (2mg/ml). Results Synthesis and characterization of blood-brain barrier-penetrating nanoparticles Previous studies have shown the various biological effects of vorinostat (the structure shown in Fig. 1 c), yet poor water solubility greatly limited its usage and biological activity [ 46 ]. To overcome this limitation, we manufactured SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 with about 110 nm by thin lipid film hydration method by the mixture of DSPE−Se−Se-PEG-Ang2, 1,2-dioleoyl-3- trimethylammonium-propane (DOTAP), 1,2-dipalmitoyl-snglycero-3-phosphocholine (DPPC) and cholesterol (Chol) (Fig. 1 a and b). As shown in Fig. 1 d and e, surface modification of the liposomes led to an increase in particle size. However, this modification has no significant effect on the surface charge of LNP (Fig. 1 f). We next evaluated the cellular uptake efficiency of SAHA@LIPO-ANG2. Flow cytometry revealed peak cellular internalization by 8 hours post treatment (Fig. 1 g-i). The in vivo fluorescence imaging following intravenous injection (Fig. 1 j) further confirms that SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 exhibits superior brain delivery efficiency. Compared to free vorinostat, the SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 formulation exhibited greater brain accumulation in mice following intravenous administration, attributable to its enhanced lipophilicity (Fig. 1 k). Notably, SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 achieves high brain enrichment (95.8%) within approximately 2 hours (Fig. 1 l and m). Additionally, we compared the cellular uptake efficiency of SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 and SAHA@LIPO materials. Results indicate that at the cellular level, SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 exhibits higher cellular uptake efficiency (Fig. S1a and b). Collectively, these results demonstrate its efficient BBB penetration and therapeutic potential. Furthermore, CCK-8 assays and H&amp;E staining confirmed good biocompatibility at a concentration of 0.25 μM (Fig. S2a and b). To validate the above hypothesis, we constructed a lentivirus encoding shRNA targeting NHE6 (Fig. S3a-c), and established stable NHE6-knockdown cells (Fig. 2 a). Flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 150 for LV-NHE6 KD-mCherry achieved optimal transfection efficiency in both BV2 and C8-D1A cells (Fig. 2 b-d). Western blotting analysis revealed that NHE6 protein expression was downregulated in cells treated with 150 MOI of lentivirus (Fig. 2 e and f). Subsequently, pH sensitive fluorescent probes were employed to detect differences in endosomal pH level. In fluorescence image statistics, one ROI is randomly selected from each image for measurement. At least 6–7 independent images are analyzed per experimental group. Compared to untreated cells, the pH level was significantly reduced in NHE6-knockdown C8-D1A and BV2 cells (Fig. 2 g-i). Compared to the control group, the NHE6 OE group exhibited significant deacidification of pH (Fig. S4d and e). However, Western blotting analysis revealed no statistically difference in LRP1 protein (Fig. 2 e and f). Thus, we hypothesize that NHE6 knockdown specifically impairs LRP1 plasma membrane expression. To investigate whether SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 modulates LRP1 expression directly or indirectly, we administered the nanoparticle to NHE6-knockdown cells. Treatment with SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 (0.25 μM, overnight) rescued the acidification-induced suppression of LRP1 plasma membrane expression, as evidenced by increased LRP1 immunofluorescence intensity and enhanced recycling of LRP1 in cells (Fig. 2 j and k, Fig. S5a and b). Interestingly, when we knocked out LRP1 in BV2 cells and treated them with 1μm fluorescent microspheres, immunofluorescence revealed that the LRP1 knockout group engulfed fewer fluorescent microspheres (Fig. S 6d and e). Consistent with this finding, overexpression of NHE6 in BV2 cells increased the number of fluorescent microspheres phagocytosed by the cells (Fig. S 4f and g). BP is marked by dark cyan, CC is marked by sienna and MF is marked by steel blue Discussion The accumulation of Aβ peptides represents a core pathological hallmark of AD. Beyond direct neurotoxicity, Aβ deposition triggers aberrant glial responses, which in turn perpetuate neuroinflammation and synaptic dysfunction [ 62 , 63 ]. In AD, elevated HDACs activity inhibits Aβ clearance [ 64 ], induces memory impairment and promotes AD progression [ 65 ]. Previous studies have hypothesized HDACs nuclear translocation suppresses the transcription of NHE6, which is a key regulator of endosomal pH. Although current AD therapeutic strategies aim to enhance Aβ clearance through various approaches, the critical link between endosomal pH dysregulation and impaired Aβ clearance has rarely been explored. Here, we focus on the “HDAC-NHE6-PH” axis, in which HDACs induce transcriptional repression through nuclear translocation, leading to reduced NHE6 expression and subsequent endosomal pH acidification. Then, LRP1 was trapped within endosomes and its plasma membrane expression was decreased which obstructs transcytotic clearance of Aβ across the blood-brain barrier [ 17 ]. As an HDAC4 inhibitor, vorinostat suppresses HDAC nuclear translocation, thereby alleviating transcription suppression caused by nuclear translocation. This supports the potential of vorinostat as a therapeutic agent for counteracting NHE6 suppression. However, its poor transmembrane permeability, off-target effects water solubility and low bioavailability significantly limit their further clinical research and application [ 66 ]. To overcome these limitations, we constructed small-molecule lipid nanoparticles capable of carrying angiopep-2 (SAHA@LIPO-ANG2) which efficiently across the blood-brain barrier and targeting LRP1. Treatment of SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 significantly reduced Aβ deposits in the brains of AD mice. Our study confirms that SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 mitigates AD pathology primarily by targeting the HDAC-NHE6-pH axis, thereby restoring glial clearance pathways. Previous studies have demonstrated that the Na/H exchanger activity of NHE6 limits endosomal acidification and regulates LRP1 receptor-mediated Aβ clearance rates [ 26 ]. Consistently, abnormal endosomal acidification in ApoE4-expressing astrocytes leads to LRP1 retention within the endoplasmic reticulum, resulting in loss of surface expression and impaired Aβ clearance [ 17 ]. In line with these findings, we observed decreased LRP1 membrane expression in NHE6-knockdown cells (Fig. 2 e), while SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 restored NHE6 expression, normalized endosomal pH (Fig. 2 g) and LRP1 plasma expression (Fig. 2 j). We speculate that increased LRP1 expression contributes to an improved inflammatory milieu in the AD brain. As expected, SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 reduces inflammatory cytokine expression in microglia, including IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. Phagocytosis plays a fundamental role in maintaining brain homeostasis by clearing cellular debris and misfolded proteins such as Aβ [ 67 ]. Impairments in phagocytic receptor expression or transport function are increasingly recognized as key contributors to neurodegenerative pathology [ 68 ], suggesting that enhancing receptor function may promote Aβ clearance and delay disease progression [ 68 ]. In NHE6-null mice, previous studies reported a significant increase in CD68⁺ staining within microglia, indicating heightened phagocytic activity [ 69 ]. On the contrary, we demonstrate that pH normalization through SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 enhances the phagocytic function of glial cells. Specifically, the treatment promoted glial cell around Aβ plaques and increases CD68⁺ phagocyte expression in AD mice (Fig. 4 a-e). We hypothesize that in NHE6 mutant mouse models, pathological changes resulting from NHE6 dysfunction induce glial proliferation, reflecting enhanced glial phagocytosis. However, in AD mice, Aβ accumulation has already triggered widespread microenvironmental alterations. SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 counteracts the impaired phagocytic recognition caused by NHE6 suppression and endosomal acidification, thereby promoting microglial aggregation around Aβ deposits. APP is widely known as the protein which originates Aβ. However, APP levels do not correlate with the clinical severity of dementia in AD patients [ 70 ]. In our study, inhibiting HDAC4 results in no changes in APP protein levels but a sustained reduction in Aβ (Fig. 4 i and 5 d), suggesting that the clearance rather than the production of Aβ is enhanced. CD36 is a member of the class B scavenger receptor family and is expressed on various cell types, including monocytes, macrophages, endothelial cells, and adipocytes [ 71 ]. CD36-related Aβ clearance by phagocytosis is another focus of microglia [ 72 ]. We found that SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 promoted CD36 expression, which may further contribute to enhanced phagocytosis by microglia (Fig. 4 i). Proinflammatory factors are known to suppress the expression of binding receptors on microglia and attenuate their phagocytic function [ 73 , 74 ]. The ability of SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 to reduce inflammatory cytokine expression in microglia may therefore explain its beneficial effect on Aβ phagocytosis SAHA@LIPO-ANG2. Besides, studies revealed that downregulation or inhibition of HDAC4 can prevent inflammatory responses induced by LPS, supporting the notion that HDAC4 represents a viable anti-inflammatory strategy for promoting Aβ clearance. Autophagy is the primary cellular pathway for clearing aggregated proteins, and upregulating autophagy serves as a rational therapeutic strategy against the excessive production of neurotoxic Aβ [ 75 ]. Aβ-dependent induction of autophagy-related gene expression enhances autophagic function in response to Aβ burden, reflecting a plastic response of astrocytic autophagy [ 24 ]. Previous work has shown that tau uptake and degradation in astrocytes were enhanced by HDAC7 inhibition or deletion [ 76 ]. Conversely, Aβ and inflammatory environments suppress autophagy-related gene expression in astrocytes, impairing efficient fusion with autophagosomes or phagosomes and resulting in dual dysfunction of phagocytosis and autophagy [ 25 ]. Furthermore, NHE6 deficiency disrupts endosomal maturation and impairs endosome-lysosome fusion, highlighting NHE6’s critical role in maintaining endosomal system integrity. Consequently, loss of NHE6 activity induces widespread lysosomal dysfunction, triggering glial inhibition and inflammatory activation that ultimately impairs Aβ clearance processes. In our study, SAHA@LIPO-ANG2 treatment significantly enhanced NHE6 expression (Fig. 2 g) and increased levels of lysosome-associated membrane protein 2 (LAMP2) (Fig. 5 f, h). Consistent with prior study [ 77 ], HDAC4 reduction restored BDNF and TGF protein levels, accompanied by improvements in neural phenotypes [ 78 ]. We propose that HDAC4 inhibition promotes the fusion of autophagosomes and lysosomes, and that reduced neuroinflammation together with NHE6 restoration leading to increased LAMP2 expression in cellular lysosomes, thereby enhancing autophagosome-lysosome fusion in astrocytes and promoting the phagocytic clearance of Aβ. Our results support the hypothesis that HDAC inhibition upregulates NHE6, promoting endosomal alkalinization and restores pH homeostasis. This, in turn, facilitates the trafficking of LRP1 to the plasma membrane and enhances Aβ clearance. Concurrently, pH normalization ameliorates lysosomal dysfunction in glial cells: it boosts microglial phagocytic capacity, evidenced by increased CD68⁺ and phagocytic receptor expression. Meanwhile, it reactivates astrocytic autophagy-lysosomal function, as indicated by elevated LAMP2 and improved autophagosome-lysosome fusion. Collectively, these mechanisms synergistically reduce Aβ burden and neuroinflammation. However, our current study has several limitations. We did not perform in vivo gene editing to validate the impact of NHE6 knockdown on endosomal pH. Although HDAC inhibitor therapy has demonstrated significant efficacy in AD treatment, its effects on the nervous system still require intuitive and rigorous evaluation. Our experimental design was relatively short-term and did not explore the optimal intervention window for HDAC inhibitors. Therefore, future studies should investigate the long-term safety and clinical potential of this system, as well as its applicability to other neurological disorders. Conclusion In summary, this study successfully developed blood-brain-barrier-targeted nanoparticles, SAHA@LIPO-ANG2for efficient delivery of the histone deacetylase inhibitor vorinostat. Our findings elucidate that the nanoparticles alleviateHDAC4-mediated repression of the NHE6 promoter, rectifying endosomal hyperacidification, and restoring plasma membrane expression of the Aβ clearance receptor LRP1. This HDAC4-NHE6-pH axis further enhances Aβ clearance through multiple synergistic mechanisms: upregulating phagocytic receptors and promoting microglial recruitment to Aβ plaques, while simultaneously reactivating astrocytic autophagy-lysosomal function through increased LAMP2 expression. In 5xFAD Alzheimer’s disease model mice, this multi-step strategy establishes a positive feedback loop that may disrupt the vicious cycle between Aβ deposition and neurodegeneration, suggesting novel therapeutic opportunities. This work not only reveals a regulatory axis in AD pathogenesis but also offers a potential pathway for restoring Aβ homeostasis. Supplementary Information Supplementary Materials 1
