Effectiveness of Souvenaid® combined with acetylcholinesterase inhibitors on caregiver burden in Alzheimer´s disease

Abstract INTRODUCTION The burden experienced by caregivers of Alzheimer´s disease (AD) patients is a complex and stressful situation influenced by distinct variables, such as the patient´s need for monitoring, social isolation, mental or physical health problems, or financial challenges. In additon, behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia are strongly associated with caregiver experiences of burden. METHODS This was a 12‐month, retrospective, single‐center, real‐world study to evaluate the effectiveness of the combined treatment with Souvenaid and acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (AChEI) compared to treatment with either AChEI alone or Souvenaid alone on caregiver burden, anxiety and depression in patients, and AD patients attending a memory clinic. Assessments were conducted using the Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI), the Beck Depression Inventory (BDI), the State Trait Anxiety Inventory‐State (STAI‐S), and the Neuropsychiatric Inventory‐Caregiver distress scale (NPI‐D). A mixed model for repeated measures was conducted to evaluate differences from baseline to month 12 between caregivers in the three treatment groups. RESULTS At 12 months, caregiver burden, depression, anxiety levels, and the emotional distress in the context of behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD) improved in caregivers in the Souvenaid plus AChEI group ( n = 70) compared to those in the AChEI group ( n = 84) and those in the Souvenaid group ( n = 66). The mean change for the ZBI score was found to be significantly higher in the Souvenaid plus AChEI group than in the AChEI group (p &lt; 0.001) and the Souvenaid group (p &lt; 0.001). In addition, there was a significant difference in the mean change for the BDI, STAI‐S, and NPI‐D scores between groups, favoring the Souvenaid plus AChEI group over the AChEI and Souvenaid groups. DISCUSSION In addition to assessing patients' cognitive, behavioral, and daily functioning, it is recommended regular monitoring of caregiver burden while providing support for patients with dementia. The combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEI may have a significant impact on caregivers’ experience of burden.

1 BACKGROUND Alzheimer's disease (AD) is one of the most challenging health and social problems today, with significant repercussions not only for people living with dementia, but also for their caregivers, families and society. 1 Findings from research have shown that caregivers of AD experience high levels of burden, anxiety and depression. 2 , 3 , 4 Caregiver burden is a complex, enduring and stressful situation influenced by distinct variables, such as the patient´s need for regular monitoring, absence of support from other relatives, mental or physical health problems, psychological distress, and financial challenges. 5 In additon, behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD) are some of the manifestations most strongly associated with caregiver experiences of burden. 2 , 6 , 7 , 8 Indeed, caregivers have indicated that BPSD are a more significant source of stress when compared with cognitive and functional symptoms. 5 , 9 The presence of BPSD means that caregivers need more daily help at home, as well as greater supervision and self‐care, leading to significant changes in their lifestyle. 10 , 11 Moreover, caregivers express a higher number of unmet personal needs and lack of time for themselves and the early institutionalisation of patients with dementia occurs as a result of burdened caregivers in more than 80% of cases. 11 , 12 Epidemiological and clinical studies show that rates of psychiatric diagnoses, especially anxiety and depression, are consistently higher in family caregivers of AD patients with BPSD than in the general population. 13 , 14 , 15 Various educational and multi‐component interventions have demonstrated consistent benefits in terms of distress, anxiety, and depression. 16 , 17 However, due to the disruptive nature of BPSD, most caregivers request pharmacological intervention at some point, in the hope that the behavioral manifestations will be instantly suppressed. In addition to antipsychotic medications, acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (AChEIs) are frequently employed in the management of BPSD such as agitation, aggression, delusions, and other related symptoms. However, meta‐analyses of the efficacy of both categories of drugs have found modest evidence of their effectiveness. 18 Souvenaid® is a medical food containing Fortasyn Connect, a combination of nutrients developed to ameliorate synaptic dysfunction. 19 , 20 , 21 Recently, we carried out a study which aimed to shed light on the potential synergistic effect of the combination of Souvenaid and AChEIs in patients with mild AD in a real‐world context. 22 For this purpose, we conducted a review of data from AD patients who attended a memory clinic over a 12‐month period. We studied patients who were treated with some of the following drugs: Souvenaid, donepezil, galantamine, or rivastigmine patch. Subjects were divided into three groups according to the treatment they had received: Souvenaid alone, AChEI alone, or Souvenaid plus AChEIs. The results showed that combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEI for 12 months revealed a significant improvement in cognitive functioning and behavioral symptomatology compared to AChEI or Souvenaid alone in patients suffering from early AD. In light of these findings, we hypothesize that, given that the combination treatment alleviates BPSD for patients it could also reduce caregiver burden. Therefore, the aim of this study is to evaluate, in a real‐world clinical setting, the effectiveness of the combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs compared to treatment with either AChEI or Souvenaid alone on caregiver burden, anxiety and depression in patients with AD. To this end, we carried out a retrospective analysis of service provision data for patients with AD who attended a memory clinic over a 12‐month period. 2 METHODS 2.1 Study design and participants This was a 12‐month, retrospective, single‐center, real‐world study to evaluate the effectiveness of the combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs compared to treatment with either AChEIs or Souvenaid alone on caregiver burden, anxiety, and depression in patients with AD. We identified family caregivers of AD patients who were attending the Alzheimer Disease Center and Memory Clinic of the Instituto Andaluz de Neurociencia (IANEC), Málaga, Spain. Patients were clinically defined in stages 3 or 4 of the Reisberg Global Deterioration Scale (GDS). 23 The diagnosis of AD was recorded in each participant's medical history and had been issued by a neurologist, neuropsychologist, or psychiatrist. Following a diagnosis of AD, patients and their family caregivers are usually assessed periodically, typically every six months. Information regarding demographic and clinical characteristics of caregivers and patients was retrieved from the IANEC medical records. The recorded clinical data collected from caregivers included burden, level of anxiety, level of depression, emotional distress caused by BPSD, and the number of hours per day spent providing care and assistance with activities of daily living. Concerning patients' data on the effects of drugs studied on cognitive functioning, BPSD, and functional capacity were collected. AD patients who received fixed and adjusted treatment for 12 months with some of the following drugs and doses were considered as candidates: Souvenaid (1 dose daily), donepezil (5 or 10 mg daily), galantamine (16 or 24 mg daily), or rivastigmine patch (9.5 or 13.3 mg daily). Subjects were divided into three groups according to the treatment they had received: Souvenaid alone, AChEI alone or Souvenaid plus AChEI. During the study period, no patients started taking any antipsychotic medication. With regard to the use of antidementia drugs, no patients modified their medication and none of them received combined treatment with AChEIs and memantine during the study period. The sample size in this retrospective study was not determined a priori. The study protocol was approved by the Málaga Research Ethics Committee. Written informed consent was obtained from both caregivers and patients who were able to give consent or their legal representatives. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards set out in the Declaration of Helsinki, as revised in its latest version (Fortaleza, Brazil, 2013). 2.2 Assessment Evaluations were carried out at three points in time: at baseline (the day of assessment and treatment initiation), at 6 months (± 1 month) and at 12 months (± 1 month). RESEARCH IN CONTEXT Systematic review : We reviewed the literature using traditional sources. Caregiver burden is a stressful situation influenced by various variables, including behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD). Research shows that BPSD are a more significant source of caregiver stress when compared with cognitive and functional symptoms. Prior studies have shown benefits of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (AChEIs) and Souvenaid in the management of BPSD. Interpretation : Our findings showed that caregivers of patients who were treated with the combination of Souvenaid and AChEIs for 12 months showed significant lower burden, anxiety and depression than those treated with Souvenaid or AChEIs alone. The benefits were independent of caregivers´ sociodemographic characteristics, and their baseline burden, anxiety or depression levels. Future directions : This study provides a valuable model for the design of future long‐term randomized controlled trials that help to support the combined use of Souvenaid and AChEIs in reducing the caregiver burden of AD patients. 2.3 Caregiver data Information on age, sex, education, relationship to the patient, marital status, number of hours per day spent giving care, overall health and whether they were living with the patient was compiled. In addition, caregivers completed: The Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI) 24 is a 22‐item self‐report questionnaire that is a measure of the subjective burden of caregivers, in terms of health, finance, and social/interpersonal relationships. Participants respond to the statements using a five‐point scale with a total score ranging from 0 to 88. Based on established cutoff scores, caregivers with ZBI scores ≥ 21 were considered as having caregiving burden. The severity of depressive symptoms was measured using the Beck Depression Inventory (BDI), 25 a 21‐item self‐report questionnaire. Each item ranges from 0 to 3 and is composed of four statements, representing increasing increments of depression severity for a particular symptom. According to the established cutoff, scores, results ≥14 are indicative of depression. The state subscale of the State‐Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI‐S) 26 was used to assess the anxiety levels of the caregivers. It comprises 20 statements which the individual's answers stating how they usually feel. Each item´s score ranges from 0 to 3, with total score ranging from 0 to 60 points; the higher the score, the higher the anxiety. We use the commonly defined cutoff of ≥ 20 to indicate the presence of anxiety. The Neuropsychiatric Inventory‐Caregiver distress scale (NPI‐D) 27 was utilised for evaluating the emotional distress experienced by caregivers in the context of BPSD. The caregiver evaluates their own level of distress on a five‐point scale ranging from 0 to 5 points (0 representing ‘not upsetting’ and 5 representing ‘extremely upsetting’). The total NPI‐D score was calculated (range 0–60). The number of hours spent by the caregiver providing care for activities of daily living (H‐ADL) was determined by obtaining Information from the relative or caregiver directly involved with the patient's everyday care. 2.4 Patient data A detailed assessment of the demographics, treatment, cognitive, functional, and BPSD measures has previously been published. 22 2.5 Statistical analysis Demographic variables were reported using the mean, standard deviation in the case of quantitative variables, and number and percentage in the case of qualitative. Baseline characteristics were compared between the three treatment groups using chi‐square test for categorical variables or one‐way analysis of variance analysis of variance (ANOVA) /Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables. A mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) analysis was carried out in order to evaluate differences from baseline to months 6 and 12 between treatment groups in burden, anxiety, depression and H‐ADL. The effect of time (between baseline measurements and each time point), treatment group and the interaction between time and treatment were evaluated. Baseline ZBI, BDI, STAI‐S, NPI‐D and H‐ADL total scores were included as covariates. Standardized effect size (SES) was also calculated for the change in clinical variable scores from baseline. The differences from baseline to months 6 and 12 were estimated using least‐squares (LS) mean (standard error [SE]) change with 95% confidence interval (CI). Post hoc analyses for multiple comparisons were conducted using Bonferroni´s correction. In addition to the analyses described above, subgroup analyses were also conducted to ascertain whether caregiver characteristics (sex, relationship to patient, marital status, overall health or caregiver´s place of residence) influenced caregiver burden, anxiety and depression. All analyses were conducted with SPSS Statistics (Version 31.0, IBM Corporation) and the significance level was set at p &lt; 0.05. 3 RESULTS We assessed a total of 220 caregivers (148 female and 72 male) who met the inclusion criteria and whose demographic and clinical data were available in the medical charts for analysis. The mean age of the sample was of 64.45 ± 5.84 years (range 52–78 years) and an average of 10.10 ± 3.40 years of education (range 6–18 years). All subjects were Caucasian. There were no differences at baseline between caregivers of patients treated with Souvenaid alone ( n = 66) and those treated with AChEI alone ( n = 84) or with Souvenaid plus AChEI ( n = 70) in relation to the clinical and demographic variables (Table 1 ). The results of the subgroup analyses were consistent with those observed in the overall study population, indicating that caregiver burden, depression, and anxiety are not influenced by caregiver characteristics. 4 DISCUSSION To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate, in real‐world clinical practice, the effectiveness of the combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs compared to treatment with either AChEI or Souvenaid alone on caregiver burden in patients with mild AD. Caregivers of patients who were treated with the combination of Souvenaid and AChEIs for 12 months showed significant lower burden, anxiety and depression than those treated with Souvenaid or AChEIs alone. The benefits were independent of caregivers´ sociodemographic characteristics, and their baseline burden, depression or anxiety levels. The combined treatment yielded a statistically significant improvement over AChEIs and Souvenaid alone after the 12‐month follow‐up in ZBI, BDI, and STAI‐S. Furthermore, the combined treatment also showed improvements already at 6 months in the same measurements. In the current study, the prevalence of caregiver burden was almost 70%. This is in agreement with data from a meta‐analysis where the prevalence ranged from 35.8 to 88.5%. 4 The mean caregiver burden score of 32.9 is in line with the results of similar studies that used the ZBI. 29 , 30 , 31 The prevalence of caregiver depressive and anxiety symptoms were 32% and 36%, respectively. Similar findings were reported by other studies with informal caregivers. 4 , 14 , 32 As we demonstrated previously, the combination of Souvenaid and AChEIs has been shown to significantly improve cognitive functioning as well as BPSD, compared to AChEIs or Souvenaid alone. 22 These findings, derived from real clinical practice, suggest that combining two drugs whose actions are based on different mechanisms could provide greater efficacy in the treatment of early AD than using each drug separately, which in turn would also result in benefits for the caregiver. As has been shown by preceding studies, BPSD are some of the main factors associated with the experience of burden among caregivers. 2 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 Therefore, interventions that target these symptoms may reduce the distress experienced by both patients and their caregivers. Our previous study showed that the combination treatment provided an improvement of treatment for BPSD compared to Souvenaid or AChEIs administered individually in mild AD. 22 These results suggest that caregivers may experience a reduction in burden when the two treatments are combined and indicate that caregiver burden is sensitive to changes in the patient's clinical condition. Our data are consistent with those reported by other real‐world studies that have shown that Souvenaid improves BPSD in patients with mild AD. One study 33 found that Souvenaid improved BPSD over a year at least in patients with MCI and dementia. Results of an observational survey showed the effectiveness of Souvenaid on cognitive symptoms of AD and MCI and also the effectiveness on behavioral and functional deficits. 34 A further study revealed improvements in depression, anxiety, and apathy. 32 On the other hand, several studies have reported a potential association between AChEIs use and improvements in overall frequency and severity of BPSD showing a trend towards enhancement or stabilization in patients with mild to moderate dementia, in comparison to those who remain untreated. 35 , 36 , 37 Therefore, these findings suggest that the reduction in caregiver burden may be associated with the improvement in BPSD that is achieved through the combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs. As research shows, symptoms of anxiety and depression are common among family caregivers of people with dementia compared with the general population, impacting on their health and quality of life while caring for a person with dementia. 3 , 4 , 14 , 22 For instance, research indicates that up to 80% of family members caring for someone with dementia also experience symptoms of depression, compared with 6% to 9% of adults over the age of 55 in the general population. 18 In the present study, most caregivers had a combination of both anxiety and depression. According to other results, 38 , 39 there is significant comorbidity between anxiety and depression in the early stages of the disease, with a high frequency of depressed caregivers who also suffer from anxiety, although the reverse is not always true. 2 , 38 , 40 A number of factors have been proposed as possible moderators of the presence of depression and anxiety in caregivers. These include living with the care recipient, the level of dependence of the patient, being a female caregiver, the quality of the relationship between caregiver and care recipient, and the health of the caregiver. 14 , 38 , 40 Moreover, the amount of time provided for the family member and the severity of the individual's dementia, has a direct impact on the levels of depression and anxiety experienced. 14 , 41 In addition, several studies have examined the relationship between caregiver burden and caregiver depressive and anxiety symptomatology. Caregiver burden was consistently found to have a significant, undeniable relationship with caregiver depressive and anxiety symptoms. 42 , 43 , 44 Thus, reducing caregiver burden could have a positive impact on symptoms of anxiety and depression, which would be consistent with the results found in this study. Importantly, the clinical implication of the statistically significant effect observed at 12‐months with the combined treatment could be ascertained by the value of the SES obtained, which reached a median of 0.59 for the combined treatment compared to AChEI alone and 0.64 for Souvenaid alone. This is in agreement with data from randomized controlled trials of Souvenaid in AD that showed that effect sizes for cognitive, functional, and behavioral outcomes tend to confirm that Souvenaid can achieve clinically evident effects in patients with mild AD. 45 , 46 The advantages of combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs showed in the present study could be attributed the potential sum of the acetylcholinesterase inhibitory effect caused by AChEI and the potential positive impact of Souvenaid on the development and functionality of synapses. The loss of brain cholinergic neurons and the deterioration of neurotransmission, which is related to reduced acetylcholinesterase activity, are both critical factors involved in the pathogenesis of AD. In this respect, the ability to inhibit the action of acetylcholinesterase is the basis for the use of AChEIs. 47 , 48 On the other hand, the specific nutrient combination of Souvenaid was specifically selected and designed to supply membrane precursors and cofactors that enhance the efficiency in phospholipid turn over, to preserve neuron membrane integrity and, consequently, extend the synaptic connectivity. 49 , 50 Taking all this into account, the benefits observed in this study as a result of combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs, rather than treatment with either of them alone, could be due to their effects being added together A strength of this study was the 1‐year follow‐up period, which suggests that the clinical benefit of Souvenaid plus AChEIs may be attributable to its long‐term use. Furthermore, all patients participating in the study underwent a comprehensive neuropsychological evaluation, using widely accepted neuropsychological assessments. Semiannual evaluations facilitated close monitoring of clinical changes. This study has certain limitations that should be considered when evaluating the validity of the findings. As this was a retrospective study, it was not possible to assign patients to treatment groups randomly, which may have affected the results obtained. Additionally, the observational nature of the study precludes the ability to determine causality. Given that this was a single‐center study, only a limited number of patients were recruited. It is recommended that further studies be conducted at multiple facilities in the future. Moreover, we considered AChEIs as a whole without focusing specifically on any of them. Therefore, clinical differences could be found if the AChEIs were studied individually. Nevertheless, the available scientific evidence does not show he superiority of any one single AChEI. 47 5 CONCLUSIONS In view of the significance of caregiver burden and its negative consequences for both the caregiver and the patient, future clinical trials should pay more attention to this crucial outcome. It is vital to reduce the distress and psychological morbidity experienced by family caregivers of people with AD, so that they can continue to provide care within the home. In addition to assessing patients' cognitive, behavioral, and daily functioning, regular monitoring of caregiver burden while providing support for patients with dementia is recommended. The combined treatment with Souvenaid and AChEIs may have a significant impact on caregivers’ experience of burden, anxiety and depression.
