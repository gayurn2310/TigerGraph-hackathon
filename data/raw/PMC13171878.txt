Preoperative frontal EEG power spectral features associated with psychomotor subtypes of postoperative delirium in cardiovascular surgery: a prospective study

Postoperative delirium (POD) is associated with complications following cardiovascular surgery, and its psychomotor subtypes differ in clinical course and treatment responsiveness, underscoring the importance of personalized preventive strategies. We aimed to investigate preoperative neurophysiological characteristics associated with POD and its psychomotor subtypes in patients undergoing cardiovascular surgery. This prospective, single-center study included 209 patients undergoing cardiovascular surgery. Preoperative frontal electroencephalography (EEG) was recorded using a patch-type device with three electrodes (left, center, and right). Standardized mean power spectral density of the following frequency bands was calculated: theta (4–8 Hz), alpha (8–13 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz), gamma1 (30–59 Hz), and gamma2 (61–75 Hz). Delirium and its psychomotor subtypes were assessed until postoperative day 7. EEG features were compared between patients with and without POD and among psychomotor subtypes, with adjustment for multiple comparisons. POD occurred in 70 patients (33.5%). No significant differences in preoperative EEG power were observed between patients with and without POD. However, patients with the hyperactive-or-mixed subtype demonstrated significantly higher theta and lower alpha activity than those with the hypoactive or no motor subtypes. These findings may identify neurophysiological markers associated with underlying psychomotor symptoms in delirium and may inform preoperative subtype risk stratification. Introduction Delirium is an acute and fluctuating disturbance of attention and consciousness that arises from a physical disorder or invasive procedure, including surgery 1 . Postoperative delirium (POD) is common among surgical patients, particularly those undergoing cardiovascular surgery—one of the most invasive surgical procedures—with reported prevalence rates of up to 64% 2 . POD after cardiovascular surgery poses serious risks to patients, including long-term cognitive impairment, decline in activities of daily living, and increased mortality 3 – 5 . Several nonpharmacological interventions, such as the Hospital Elder Life Program 6 , have been investigated to address this issue, but they require substantial staffing and financial resources to be applied to all patients in clinical practice 7 . Therefore, early prediction and identification of individuals at high risk of POD is essential 8 . Although inflammatory and metabolic factors have attracted increasing attention as potential biomarkers of delirium, their clinical application remains challenging because of the fluctuating nature of the condition and its overlap with other physical illnesses 9 . In contrast, electroencephalography (EEG) has long been used to detect delirium, with characteristic findings including increased slow-wave activity and reduced alpha power 10 . Previous research suggests that EEG could detect early neuroelectrical changes that occur before the behavioral symptoms of delirium. These changes may reflect cellular and chemical alterations induced by stress that disrupt brain function 11 . Nevertheless, only a limited number of studies have examined EEG findings before the onset of delirium 10 . Delirium is generally classified into four groups based on psychomotor symptoms: hyperactive, hypoactive, mixed, and no motor 12 . These subtypes exhibit distinct clinical profiles, and identifying them is essential for the effective prevention of POD 13 . For example, a recent clinical trial suggested that the prophylactic use of suvorexant to prevent POD may be particularly effective in hyperactive delirium. In contrast, hypoactive delirium may be more susceptible to the adverse synergistic effects of anesthetics and sedatives administered during the perioperative period 14 . However, to our knowledge, the neurophysiological differences among these subtypes remain unclear, and predicting the development of a specific delirium subtype in the preoperative period remains challenging. We aimed to investigate the neurophysiological activity associated with the development of POD and its psychomotor subtypes through preoperative EEG measurements in patients undergoing cardiovascular surgery. First, we compared preoperative EEG oscillatory features between patients with and without POD. Second, among patients with POD, we compared EEG features across the different psychomotor subtypes. Results Participants and prevalence of delirium Of the 253 patients who met the inclusion criteria and provided written informed consent, 209 (median [interquartile range (IQR)] age at enrollment, 71 [63–76] years; 140 [67.0%] male) were ultimately included in the analysis (Fig. 1 ). Among these, 70 patients (33.5%) developed POD within 7 days after surgery. The distribution of psychomotor subtypes among patients with POD was 14 (20.0%), 30 (42.9%), and 26 (37.1%), in the no motor subtype, hypoactive subtype, and hyperactive-or-mixed subtype, respectively (Fig. 1 ). Of the 26 patients in the hyperactive-or-mixed group, 6 had pure hyperactive delirium, and 20 had mixed delirium. Post hoc sensitivity analyses Sensitivity analyses excluding patients using preoperative benzodiazepines demonstrated preserved subtype-specific differences in the alpha band ( η 2 = 0.197–0.213, P &lt; 0.01), with consistent medium-to-large pairwise effect sizes. Theta effects were attenuated but remained directionally consistent (Supplementary Tables 4 and Supplementary Fig. 1 ). Discussion Our study demonstrated two main findings: (1) baseline EEG oscillatory features were similarly distributed in patients with and without POD, regardless of clinical background, and (2) patients who developed the hyperactive-or-mixed subtype of delirium exhibited higher preoperative theta activity and lower alpha activity compared with those who developed the hypoactive or no motor subtype, despite comparable clinical characteristics across the three groups. These findings suggest that preoperative EEG features may be associated with the neurophysiological mechanisms underlying the development of psychomotor symptoms in POD among cardiovascular surgery patients. Few studies have examined EEG characteristics prior to the onset of delirium. Consistent with a previous study 15 , we found that patients who developed POD showed preoperative EEG power spectral characteristics similar to those who did not develop POD. Effect sizes were consistently small and did not suggest clinically relevant medium effects, indicating that insufficient power alone is unlikely to explain the negative findings. Although age, baseline cognitive function, and preoperative benzodiazepine use differed between groups, multivariable analyses demonstrated that none of the PSD features independently predicted delirium outcome in the present study. A prior study, which investigated EEG dynamics during the pre- to postoperative period, reported that the progression of slow-wave activity from frontal to posterior brain regions contributed to the occurrence of delirium with an associated reduction in structural connectivity 16 . These observations imply that EEG power spectral analyses alone may be insufficient to capture the neurophysiological vulnerability to delirium onset. In our cohort, overall POD occurrence was more strongly associated with established clinical factors such as age and baseline cognitive function. On the contrary, our study highlights the potential utility of EEG power spectral features as novel markers that may help elucidate the pathophysiological basis of diverse psychomotor expressions in POD. The hyperactive-or-mixed delirium subtype—which presents with restlessness and hyperarousal symptoms—showed higher preoperative theta activity and lower alpha activity compared with the hypoactive subtype, with moderate effect sizes that persisted after sensitivity analyses excluding benzodiazepine users. Importantly, these theta and alpha differences were observed consistently across all three electrodes, supporting the robustness of the findings. The EEG pattern of increased theta and decreased alpha is commonly observed in patients with delirium symptoms 10 . Notably, our study is the first to demonstrate that this characteristic pattern was already present in preoperative EEG recordings of patients who later developed hyperactive-or-mixed POD, suggesting that vulnerability to postoperative delirium may exist before surgery. Similar spectral slowing has been associated with intrinsic functional dysconnectivity, altered cerebral perfusion, and cholinergic dysfunction in various neurocognitive conditions 17 – 19 . These observations provide a potential neurophysiological framework for interpreting the spectral pattern identified in our cohort. Our findings raise the possibility that baseline differences in theta and alpha activity reflect pre-existing neurophysiological variations that may contribute to subtype-specific manifestations of POD, and that such spectral features could have utility in preoperative risk stratification. Recent studies in intensive care settings have shown that automated continuous EEG analyses incorporating spectral, coherence, and reactivity features can serve as diagnostic classifiers for delirium in septic patients 20 , 21 . Although these studies focused on diagnostic classification rather than preoperative prediction, they support the broader rationale for investigating quantitative EEG features as potential markers. POD developed in 33.5% of our cohort, which falls within the wide range reported in the previous study (3–64%) 2 . Variability in incidence likely reflects the challenges in delirium diagnosis, including the potential for underdetection. In our study, the prospective design with direct, face-to-face assessments allowed for a more accurate assessment of delirium occurrence. We recognize several limitations in this study. First, it was conducted at a single hospital in Japan and included only patients who underwent cardiovascular surgery. The distribution of psychomotor activity subtypes varies across different populations 22 , limiting the generalizability of our findings. Nevertheless, the consistency of moderate effect sizes across analyses supports the robustness of subtype-specific alpha alterations. Additional multicenter studies involving more diverse patient populations may further clarify the mechanistic basis of these EEG differences. Second, the EEG device we used recorded only frontal signals, making it impossible to assess global EEG metrics such as occipital oscillations and functional connectivity between brain regions. However, the simplicity and practicality of the device may enhance its potential for clinical implementation, particularly in predicting specific POD subtypes. Third, delirium severity was not assessed in this study because commonly used tools, such as the Delirium Rating Scale–Revised–98 23 and the Memorial Delirium Assessment Scale 24 , would impose excessive burden on patients immediately after cardiovascular surgery. Further research is needed to investigate the dose-dependent relationship between preoperative EEG power spectral features and delirium severity using noninvasive and patient-friendly tools, such as portable EEG systems 25 . In addition, the same trained raters conducted both the preoperative assessment and the postoperative delirium evaluations. Although standardized diagnostic criteria were used, the lack of blinding may have introduced potential assessment bias. Finally, the present study did not evaluate long-term clinical outcomes according to delirium subtype. Given prior evidence linking hypoactive delirium to worse prognosis 22 , further investigation into distinct mechanisms underlying hypoactive delirium is warranted. Methods Design and population This single-center, prospective observational study was conducted at The University of Osaka Hospital on patients aged 40 years or older who underwent cardiovascular surgery between November 2021 and March 2025. We restricted participation to individuals aged ≥ 40 years because previous research has demonstrated that the prevalence of delirium is very low in populations younger than 40 years 26 . All patients were ethnically Asian and Japanese-speaking. Eligible patients underwent coronary artery bypass grafting, valve surgery, or graft replacement of the aorta via either median sternotomy or MICS, with or without cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB). Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) those with preoperative delirium; (2) those diagnosed with dementia before surgery; (3) those who required mechanical ventilation for more than 4 days, as most POD occurs within the first three postoperative days and reliable assessment of delirium is difficult during prolonged sedation; (4) cerebral hemorrhage or stroke within 7 days after surgery; (5) reoperation within 7 days after surgery; and (6) those with suspected alcohol withdrawal delirium. Alcohol withdrawal delirium was defined as consumption of ≥ 60 g of alcohol per day immediately before admission, with the onset of delirium occurring within 2 weeks of admission. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The study protocol was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of The University of Osaka Hospital (No. 21158-3; 2021/09/11) and registered with the UMIN Clinical Trial Registry (UMIN000049390). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Preoperative assessments and EEG measurements Demographic and clinical data were extracted from the patients’ electronic medical records. In addition, two trained nurses (C.N. and R.M.) visited the patients on any day between hospital admission and surgery to assess baseline cognitive function using the Japanese version of the Mini-Cog 27 . The Mini-Cog is a brief cognitive test that combines a simple memory recall task and a clock-drawing task, and it has demonstrated greater sensitivity than the Mini-Mental State Examination 27 . EEG measurements were also obtained at the bedside during the preoperative visit. A 2-minute eyes-closed, resting-state EEG was recorded using a multichannel, patch-type sensor (“HARU-1”), which consists of a wireless sensing unit and a disposable electrode sheet. The electrode sheet contains three integrated electrodes (left, center, and right) and is attached to the patient’s forehead. The device meets the international performance standard for electroencephalographs (IEC 80601-2-26) and has been approved for medical use by the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency of Japan and validated against conventional clinical EEG standards (Certification No. 302AFBZX00079000; Class II, EEG; approval date: August 7, 2020). Although formal head-to-head validation against full-scale clinical EEG systems is limited, the device has been utilized in prior peer-reviewed studies in dementia and sleep research, demonstrating stable acquisition of EEG features 28 , 29 . Before recording, the level of hum noise was checked. If it exceeded 5 µV, the recording environment was adjusted. Patients were then seated comfortably and instructed to close their eyes, after which EEG recording was initiated. The sampling rate was set at 250 Hz. No patients were excluded due to poor EEG signal quality. Signal quality was ensured by visually inspecting all recordings for excessive artifacts or abnormal waveforms. Minor transient artifacts were not strictly excluded, as the preprocessing strategy was intentionally designed to reflect real-world clinical conditions. Delirium and psychomotor subtype assessments All patients underwent daily delirium assessments starting on the day of extubation and continuing until postoperative day 7. Assessments were initiated after recovery from sedation and clinical stabilization following extubation, typically beginning approximately 3 h after extubation based on chart documentation. Because extubation was performed after successful spontaneous breathing trials and spontaneous awakening trials, patients were considered sufficiently awake for evaluation; therefore, no formal Richmond Agitation–Sedation Scale threshold was applied. Assessments were performed by two trained nurses (C.N. or R.M.) under the supervision of a certified psychiatrist (M.H.). The same rater who assessed each patient’s preoperative condition also conducted the postoperative delirium assessment to evaluate changes from the preoperative state. Electronic medical records were reviewed throughout the follow-up period, and additional in-person assessments were performed after discharge from the ICU. Delirium diagnoses were determined through direct clinical assessment, including patient interviews and bedside observations. Evaluations included assessment of orientation, attention, level of arousal, perceptual disturbances (e.g., hallucinations), and recall of postoperative events, in accordance with the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition 1 . The Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU or the Intensive Care Delirium Screening Checklist was reviewed as supportive information when they were available in the medical records. When delirium was suspected, patients were further classified into three psychomotor subtypes according to the Delirium Motor Subtype Scale (DMSS) 12 : no motor subtype, hypoactive subtype, and hyperactive-or-mixed subtype. The DMSS classifies delirium based on predominant motor features observed during the preceding 24 h, with hypoactive subtype defined by reduced motor activity and decreased responsiveness, hyperactive subtype defined by increased motor activity or agitation, mixed subtype defined by the presence of both features, and no motor subtype defined by the absence of neither. Pure hyperactive delirium was rare in our cohort, resulting in small subgroup sizes. Therefore, the hyperactive and mixed subtypes were combined into a single group following previous studies 14 , 30 . This grouping strategy was defined prior to statistical analyses of EEG outcomes to ensure analytical stability. Final assessments regarding delirium onset and subtype classification were discussed among two or more raters, including psychiatrists (M.H. or Y.M.). Considering the diurnal fluctuation characteristic of delirium, in-person assessment hours were restricted to between 3:30 pm and 6:30 pm. EEG data handling From the recorded 2-minute EEG data, a continuous 110-second segment was extracted. The first 10 s were excluded to minimize start-of-recording artifacts and to analyze a stable eyes-closed resting segment. We then applied a 4–75 Hz band-pass filter and a 60 Hz notch filter to remove powerline interference. EEG signals were standardized by converting each channel’s time-series data into z-scores prior to PSD computation, using the mean and standard deviation of the entire recording for each participant. Subsequently, the PSD was calculated for each channel, and the mean PSD was computed for the following frequency bands: theta (4–8 Hz), alpha (8–13 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz), gamma1 (30–59 Hz), and gamma2 (61–75 Hz). Python version 3.8.19 was used for EEG data analyses. Statistical analyses Our first aim was to compare EEG characteristics between patients with and without delirium. Prior to comparisons of continuous variables, the Shapiro–Wilk test was conducted, and none of the distributions met the assumption of normality. We therefore used the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous demographic and clinical variables, including age, Mini-Cog score, operation time, and duration of intensive care. For the mean PSD analysis, Bonferroni corrections were applied to comparisons across the three EEG electrodes within each frequency band. Because frequency bands (theta, alpha, beta, gamma1, and gamma2) were predefined based on established physiological classifications, they were treated as independent hypothesis families, and no additional correction across bands was applied. The same correction strategy was used in the psychomotor subtype analyses when comparisons were performed across electrodes within a given frequency band. Our second aim was to compare EEG characteristics across the three psychomotor subtypes of delirium. The Kruskal–Wallis test, followed by the Steel–Dwass post hoc test, was used to compare continuous variables, including mean PSDs. As the Steel–Dwass procedure adjusts for multiple pairwise comparisons, no further correction was applied. Multivariable logistic regression models were constructed to assess the independent association between preoperative PSD and POD, adjusting for clinical preoperative variables significant in univariable analyses. Because this was a prospective study, the sample size was determined by the number of eligible patients during the study period. To address concerns regarding statistical power, effect sizes and corresponding 95% confidence intervals were calculated for all PSD comparisons. Cliff’s delta was used for Mann–Whitney U tests, and eta squared ( η 2 ) for Kruskal–Wallis tests. Confidence intervals for η 2 were estimated using 2,000 bootstrap resamples. Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical variables—sex, preoperative benzodiazepine use, MICS, and CPB use—in both the two-group and three-group comparisons. All tests were two-tailed with a significance level of P &lt; 0.05. Continuous variables were presented as medians and IQRs, and categorical variables as counts and percentages. No missing data were observed for any variable. R software version 4.5.0 was used for statistical analyses. Post hoc sensitivity analysis Although preoperative benzodiazepine use did not significantly differ among psychomotor subtypes, a higher proportion was observed in the hyperactive-or-mixed group. Given the known effects of benzodiazepines on alpha suppression and theta-alpha balance, sensitivity analysis excluding benzodiazepine users was performed to assess the robustness of subtype-specific findings. Conclusions Our study suggests that increased preoperative theta activity and decreased alpha activity in frontal EEG oscillations are associated with hyperactive symptoms of POD in cardiovascular surgery patients, independent of clinical background. These findings may help elucidate the neurophysiological markers associated with the development of each psychomotor symptom in POD and may inform preoperative subtype risk stratification of delirium subtypes.
